validate the hue-spread metric, which resolves the sweep contradiction

The hue-spread sweep reported that `row_chroma_error` and `row_chroma_phase` changed hue
spread by exactly 0.0000 across three values each, while the same configuration measured
through the renderer showed a 0.066 mean absolute pixel difference. Both cannot be right,
so the metric got the same treatment as the displacement tracker: validate against a case
whose answer is known before trusting it.

Measured by rotating the hue of a known fraction of rows by a known angle:

    untouched                    0.4230
    10% of rows rotated 30 deg   0.4266   (+0.0037)
    10% of rows rotated 90 deg   0.7207   (+0.2977)
    30% of rows rotated 90 deg   1.0698   (+0.6469)
    100% of rows rotated 90 deg  0.4030   (-0.0200)

The metric responds correctly, so the sweep harness was wrong and its two zero rows should
not be relied on. Re-measured through the renderer, `row_chroma_error` moves the metric by
0.0252 -- real, and an order of magnitude smaller than a 90-degree rotation across a tenth
of the rows, because it rotates scattered individual rows by angles around 60 degrees with
smoothing. So the mechanism works; it is simply not the lever that explains the gap.

Which leaves `format.chroma_bandwidth` as the dominant one, at a delta of 0.9125 against a
gap of 0.114. That conclusion survives the resolution, since it never depended on the two
bad rows.

Three assertions added, and two of them teach something the note did not:

  * the metric responds to a rotation of some rows;
  * it does *not* widen when every row is rotated -- the asymmetry is what separates a
    per-line chroma fault from a global colour shift, which is the distinction the whole
    hue-spread comparison rests on;
  * a 30-degree rotation of a tenth of the rows moves it by 0.004, inside the noise of a
    fingerprint, so a sweep reporting "no effect" may simply be looking at too small a
    change.

Two of the three failed on first writing, both because the synthetic pattern was wrong
rather than the metric: a wide palette gave a base spread of 1.3670 against the
reference's 0.4230, leaving no room for a rotation to show, and the uniform-rotation
assertion demanded the value stay near the base when the correct claim is that it must not
increase. Both corrections are recorded in the check.

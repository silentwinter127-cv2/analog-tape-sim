add a midtone-and-shadow depth mechanism, and find that it does not recover the blue either

**The reasoning it was built on, and the measurement that refutes it.** The pole's blue is diluted by the *total*
neutral light added around it, and turning the glow down costs the line's contrast -- so the contrast was to be raised
from the other side, by **darkening the surround** instead of brightening the line, which adds no light to the pole and
so dilutes nothing. The mechanism does what it says: `gain(L) = 1 - a*(1-L)**2`, **exactly 1 at L = 0 and at L = 1**,
so no highlight is tinted and no shadow is lifted.

    shadow_depth   pole contrast   pole hue   blown     mean luma
    0.00           0.2183          +0.0000    0.04051   0.12698
    0.25           0.2155          +0.0000    0.03963   0.10424
    0.50           0.2101          +0.0000    0.03876   0.08555
    0.75           0.1773          +0.0000    0.03797   0.07371
    1.00           0.1569          +0.0000    0.03706   0.06511

**It lowers the pole's contrast instead of raising it, and leaves the hue at exactly zero.** Both are wrong, and the
second one is the more informative.

**Why the contrast falls is a property of the mechanism and of the frame.** It compresses the **whole** picture's
midtones and shadows -- the mean luma drops from 0.127 to 0.065 -- and the pole, being a bright line on a wall, has its
surroundings and its own lower values pulled down **together**, so the difference shrinks. **The reasoning treated the
pole as untouched because its top is at unity gain, but a line in a photograph is not only its peak: most of its pixels
are below white, and those are compressed with everything else.**

**And the hue staying at exactly +0.0000 through five values and two mechanisms is the substantive finding.** Three
independent levers have now been applied to the pole's colour -- the glow amount, `halation_source_colour`, and
`shadow_depth` -- and **none moves it by a measurable amount**. That says the quantity is not being set by the
mechanisms at all. The pole's measured hue is a **median over the brightest column of each row**, and the values along
that column are a mixture of the pole, the wall behind it and the sky; the chain's own stage-by-stage figures put the
pole well below white, so the pixels carrying the measurement are **not** the highlights the glow mechanisms act on.

**So the right next step is not another mechanism but a better instrument**: the current statistic cannot distinguish
"the pole's blue" from "the sky and wall behind the pole's brightest column", and until it can, every mechanism tried
will measure as either inert or harmful for reasons unrelated to the colour. That is the honest conclusion of three
failed attempts, and it replaces the guess that was driving them.

**Two mechanisms were added this session** -- `source_glow` and `halation_source_colour` -- and a third,
`shadow_depth`, is in place with its endpoints exact but **is not recommended for use**: at every value it lowers the
contrast it was built to raise. All three are inert at their defaults; the second is verified bit-identical; the
reproduction file carries `shadow_depth` at 0.0.

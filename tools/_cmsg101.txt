replace the pole statistic with one that measures the pole, and reverse three conclusions with it

**The instrument was the problem, and that is now demonstrated rather than suspected.** The pole in the reference scene
is **6 px wide in a 452-px frame**. The statistic used through several turns took, for each row, the **single brightest
column** and read its hue -- so it sampled a strip through the pole *and* the wall and sky beside it, and reported
whichever dominated. The instrument now:

  * **locates the pole as an object**, as the column band whose brightness stands above its horizontal neighbours
    **consistently over many rows** -- which is what a pole is and what a wall is not;
  * measures its **width** (6 px, columns 270..275, over 186 rows below the core);
  * takes the hue over **all the band's pixels**, as a median of per-row medians;
  * and takes the **same measurement on the surround** at a fixed offset, because a hue alone says nothing without its
    local reference.

**It can tell subjects apart**, which is the check that it is measuring what it claims: the reference scene gives an
excess of **+0.0450**, a raw lamp photograph **+0.0189**, and the sodium snow scene **-0.0657** -- correctly
identifying the one with no cool pole.

**And with it, three conclusions from the previous turns are reversed or corrected:**

**1. The line was never too faint -- it was over-distinct.** The reproduction's excess is **+0.1069** against the
reference's **+0.0450**, i.e. **more than twice**. The "shallow line" that three mechanisms were built to deepen was an
artefact of the diluted statistic.

**2. `source_glow` is the lever, and the direction is down, not up.** It moves the excess from **-0.0930** at 0.00 --
where the pole goes **darker than the wall around it**, the opposite of the reference -- to **+0.1060** at 0.04 and
**+0.0857** at 0.15. It is the only mechanism here that lifts the surroundings *around* a source rather than adding to
the source itself.

**3. `halation_source_colour` moves the excess by 0.0008 across its whole range, and `shadow_depth` lowers it at every
value.** Both are recorded as measured. The first is the mechanism added last turn to recover the pole's blue; it is
correct in itself -- verified bit-identical at its default and inheriting the source's colour when asked -- but **it is
not a lever on this quantity**, and the earlier expectation that it was is refuted.

**What the final setting is, and one limitation of the instrument that is stated rather than papered over.** With
`source_glow` at **0.04** the excess is **+0.1060** and the pole's contrast **0.2596**, against the reference's
**+0.0450** and **0.2237**. No value reaches the reference's excess exactly: it runs from -0.0930 at zero to a peak of
+0.1060 and back to +0.0857 at 0.15, so the reference's +0.0450 would need a value **between 0.00 and 0.02** -- where
the pole's own hue is still **-0.0379**, i.e. its sign has not yet crossed. **So the instrument's excess cannot be
matched while the pole's hue is right, and the two disagree about what "the same" means.** The likely cause is that
the band is located **once, on the source**, and the mechanisms shift the pole's brightness profile, so the fixed band
and the surround offset are not measuring the same thing the source's did. **Matching one number while the other
diverges is not a solved measurement**, and the value is set at the peak of the excess rather than pretending the
reference was reached.

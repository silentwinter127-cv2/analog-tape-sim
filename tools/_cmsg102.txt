locate the pole on every frame, and find that it was never measuring the pole

**The fix that exposed a worse fault than the one it was meant to cure.** The band had been located once on the source
and reused, which was wrong because the mechanisms change the picture. So it was made to locate independently on every
frame by the same rule -- and that rule, applied to the renders, finds the pole somewhere else entirely:

    the reference scene        band 270..275,  6 px wide, 186 rows
    source_glow = 0.00         band 307..322, 16 px wide
    source_glow = 0.04         band 308..322, 15 px wide
    source_glow = 0.15         band 310..323, 14 px wide

**The renders' band is neither in the pole's columns nor the pole's width.** So the detector is locking onto a
**different object** -- something around column 308 that stands above its horizontal neighbours over many rows, most
plausibly the building's edge or the lit window line -- and every "excess" figure quoted in the previous turn was a
comparison between **a pole in one picture and a wall in another.**

**That invalidates the readings, and it also explains the contradiction that prompted this work.** The excess and the
pole's hue disagreed about which `source_glow` was "the same" as the reference because they were not measuring the same
object at all -- not because the two quantities were in tension. The earlier explanation, that the fixed band and the
surround offset no longer described the same thing, was **right in kind and too mild in degree**.

**What is established, and what is now not.**

  * established: the pole exists and is measurable **on the source** -- 6 px at columns 270..275, pole hue **+0.0062**,
    surround **-0.0388**, excess **+0.0450**, contrast **0.2237**, over 186 rows;
  * established: **the detector's rule is too weak** to distinguish a pole from a bright vertical edge, so it cannot be
    used unconstrained;
  * **not established: anything about the renders' pole.** The three mechanisms were re-swept last turn against a
    number that was not the pole's, so **those conclusions are withdrawn** -- including the reversal that said the
    line was "over-distinct". The reversal may be right or wrong; it is unmeasured.

**So the honest state of this thread is that its instrument has failed twice and neither failure was noticed until the
second fix was applied.** The first failure was a diluted statistic that reported a line too faint to see; the second
was a detector that silently measured something else. **Both produced confident numbers.** What they have in common is
that neither had been checked against a case where the answer was known -- which is the same discipline that caught the
head-switching band and the `source_glow` magnitude earlier in this work, and it was not applied here.

**The next attempt must therefore start with a positive control**: a synthetic frame containing a known pole of known
width and known hue, over a wall of known brightness, on which the detector's location, width and colour can all be
checked against the answer before it is pointed at a photograph. Until the detector passes that, **no figure it
produces about a render is evidence.**

`tools/dynamic_pole.py` is kept: it is what found this, and its detector is the thing to fix rather than to discard.
`tools/ntscrs_ref/repro-highlight.json` stands at the previous turn's values, which are **not** validated by the
withdrawn measurements -- the reproduction renders and is clean, but the claim that its pole matches the reference is

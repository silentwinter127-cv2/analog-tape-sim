build a positive control for the pole detector, and find that every render measurement was taken off the pole

**The control, and it decided the question.** A synthetic frame with a pole of **known width, known columns and known
hue** on a wall of known brightness, plus the two cases that had gone wrong:

    a pole on a plain wall              found at 200..205, width 6, hue +0.2300   -- all correct
    a pole plus a bright edge at 330    unconstrained: picks 330..335, hue +0.0000  -- WRONG
                                        constrained:   picks 200..205              -- correct
    a wall with no pole at all          NOT FOUND                                  -- correct

**So the detector is fooled by a bright vertical edge elsewhere on the wall**, which is exactly what it did on the real
renders, and **it does not invent a pole where there is none**. The first property makes it unusable unconstrained; the
second means it is not worse than useless. **Neither was known before, and both came from a case with a known answer.**

**And with the geometry then fixed from the source -- columns 270..275, surround +/-24, the detector's only remaining
job being to check the band still clears its surround -- the decisive figure appears:**

    the source, at columns 270..275      excess +0.0363   (pole +0.0062 against surround -0.0388)

**Every render measurement in the last several turns was taken at columns 308..322.** The detector, unconstrained,
locked onto a bright vertical edge; the search window, which was meant to prevent that, did not, because **inside the
window the reproduction's frame has no pole standing above its surroundings where the source's does** -- so the
strongest remaining vertical structure in that window won. **Those figures were therefore comparing a pole in one
picture against a wall in another, and all of them are withdrawn**: the `source_glow` sweep, the
`halation_source_colour` sweep, the `shadow_depth` sweep, and the reversal that said the line was "over-distinct".

**What is established now, and it is a much smaller set than before.**

  * the pole on the source is **6 px at columns 270..275, pole hue +0.0062, surround -0.0388, excess +0.0363**;
  * **the detector must be given the geometry, not asked to find it** -- asking it to locate the pole on a frame the
    mechanisms have altered makes the measurement follow the thing being measured, which is the design fault behind
    both failed instruments;
  * and a **positive control must precede any figure about a render**, which is the discipline that caught the
    head-switching band and the `source_glow` magnitude earlier and was not applied here.

**And one thing the corrected geometry suggests but does not yet establish**: the fact that the pole does not clear its
surround in that window on the renders is consistent with the glow burying it, which is what the earlier turns
suspected for other reasons. It is **not** measured -- the locked-geometry sweep was not completed, because the script's
earlier half was left in place and printed the withdrawn figures again. **That is the first thing to run next**, and it
is a single script rather than a new line of investigation.

**A note on how this went.** Three instruments produced confident numbers here and two were wrong in ways that took
another fix to notice. The common cause is the same in both: **a statistic was trusted before it was checked against a
case whose answer was known.** `tools/pole_control.py` is kept, and it is the artefact that should have existed before
the first measurement rather than after the third.

163 checks pass, selftest clean. Three mechanisms were added this session (`source_glow`,
`halation_source_colour`, `shadow_depth`), all inert at their defaults and the second verified bit-identical.
`repro-highlight.json` stands as rendered but **without any validated claim about how its pole compares to the
reference**.

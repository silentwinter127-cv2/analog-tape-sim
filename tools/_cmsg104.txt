**And it is not burial.** The visibility check looks for rows where the band clears its surround by at least 0.02, and
the renders return **164 to 193 of about 333 rows** -- comparable to the source's **183 of 333**. So the band is
present and its *brightness* relationship holds over a similar number of rows; **what differs is the pole's colour
relative to its surroundings**, and specifically its sign: **+0.0062 against -0.0388** in the source, **0.0000 against
+0.0354** in the reproduction. **The chain is making the pole warmer than its surround rather than cooler.**

**That is a concrete, reproducible fault and it is not attributable to any parameter**, which is what the sweep
establishes -- all ten mechanisms were varied across their ranges and the sign never changed. It is also a different
statement from the one that prompted this line of work: the report was that the line looked too faint and lacked the
rainbow, and what is actually wrong at the pole is that **its colour sense is reversed relative to its surroundings**.

**Two instruments were discarded getting here, and the control is what made this reading trustworthy.** The first took
the brightest column per row and reported a line too faint to see; the second located the pole by a rule that a bright
edge defeats, and reported a wall. **Both produced confident numbers, and neither had been checked against a case with
a known answer.** The positive control -- a synthetic pole of known width, columns and hue, with a bright edge elsewhere
and a bare-wall case -- is now the artefact that precedes measurement rather than follows it, and it is kept as
`tools/pole_control.py`.

**What is established, precisely:**

  * the source's pole: **6 px, columns 270..275, excess +0.0363, contrast 0.2237**;
  * the renders' pole: **excess negative at all settings**, so the pole is **warmer than its surround** where the
    source has it **cooler**;
  * hence **no parameter setting reproduces the reference's pole**, and the search for a value was looking in a space
    that does not contain the answer.

**The next step is therefore a mechanism question, not a value question**: something in the chain is removing the
pole's cool offset, and it does so regardless of the ten settings. The candidates are the mechanisms that act on chroma
regardless of brightness -- the chroma bandwidth limits, the crosstalk terms, and the display's saturation, none of
which were varied in this sweep because they are at the reproduction's values rather than being light mechanisms.
**That is where to look, and it is a smaller question than the one being asked before.**

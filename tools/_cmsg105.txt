**So the inversion is the display stage's**, and it is not the saturation: turning saturation from 0.0 to 1.0 leaves
every figure identical to four decimals, which is itself a finding -- **the parameter that scales chroma does not move a
measurement that is entirely about chroma**, so something else in the display is dominating it.

**And the mechanism is visible in the two hues.** The display moves the pole from -0.0578 to 0.0000 and the **surround
from -0.0618 to +0.0319** -- i.e. it shifts the **dark and mid-tone** region **+0.062 in B-R** while shifting the
brighter pole by **+0.058**. Both are lifted, but the surround is lifted slightly more, and since the surround starts
below the pole the two **cross over**. So the display is adding blue to the picture in a **brightness-dependent** way,
and the amount is just enough to put the wall above the pole.

**That is a narrow and testable statement about the display stage**, and it is much smaller than the question that was
being asked before: **not "which of ten mechanisms loses the pole's blue" but "which term in the display adds
brightness-dependent blue, and why does `saturation` not govern it".** The candidates are the tube's own colour terms
-- convergence, `crt_age`, the shadow mask, the colour temperature of the display, and the tone curve -- none of which
were neutralised in this run **except through `enabled = 0`**, which removes all of them at once.

**So the bandwidth is not involved at all** -- the narrowest limit and no limit give the same figures to four decimals,
which also rules out the resolution explanation this run was built to test: **a 6-px object is not losing its colour to
a chroma lowpass, because widening the channel sixfold changes nothing.**

**And removing the chroma entirely still leaves the pole warmer than its surround** (-0.0146), so the inversion is not
produced by the chroma channel either -- it persists with chroma gone. What the chroma does is **lift both regions**,
the **surround by about 0.040** and the **pole by about 0.022**, i.e. **unequally and in the direction that makes the
inversion worse**.

**And one figure stands out as a symptom rather than a result: the pole's B-R is exactly `+0.0000` in every
configuration tried.** Across the bandwidth sweeps, the crosstalk sweeps, the saturation sweep, the tone-curve grid, and
now this -- **including settings that change the surround by 0.06** -- the pole reads **identically zero**. A quantity
that lands on exactly zero regardless of what is done to it is **being clamped or saturated**, not computed. The pole
is a bright thin line, and something in the chain is driving its chroma to null at high luminance while leaving the
dark wall's chroma intact.

**That is a substantially narrower and more concrete statement than anything before it in this line of work**, and it
reframes the search: not "which parameter loses the pole's blue" but "**what sets a bright pixel's chroma to exactly
zero**". The candidates are the terms that act on highlights specifically -- the roll-off, the clipping at the display
input, and any per-channel ceiling -- none of which this session has examined, because every sweep so far has varied
the mechanisms that act on *how light is added* rather than on **what happens to a channel that is already high**.

**What is established, and it is now a closed set of exclusions:**

  * the pole's inversion is caused by the **display stage** (disabling it restores the sign; nothing else does);
  * within the display it is carried by **`brightness`/`contrast`**, which cannot reach the reference at any setting;
  * it is **not** the chroma bandwidth (both stages, full range), **not** the crosstalk, **not** the codec, **not** any of
    the ten light mechanisms, **not** `color_temp`, **not** `saturation`, and **not** the chroma channel itself (the
    inversion survives `chroma_gain = 0`);
  * **`crt_age`, `convergence` and `mask_depth` do not reach the output at all**;
  * and **the pole's B-R is exactly zero in every configuration**, which is the new lead.

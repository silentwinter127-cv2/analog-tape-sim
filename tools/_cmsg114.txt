build the physical layer of the night-vision modes, measured against the reference clip

**The reference clip was segmented first, because it contains more than one mode.** Its mean channel ratio swings across
frames -- R:G:B **0.441:0.303:0.255** at frame 1, **0.530:0.281:0.189** at 21, then **0.294:0.456:0.249** at 41 and
**0.266:0.527:0.206** at 61 -- so averaging it produced a hue spread of 0.1549 that describes none of them. Classified
per frame, the clip has **four segments**:

    frames    mode class        G share       hue spread    hf (noise)
    0-20      colour, warm     0.28-0.30     0.24-0.29     0.0063
    24-59     green            0.44-0.55     0.06-0.11     0.010-0.019
    60-131    green            0.43-0.58     0.06-0.18     0.003-0.013
    132-142   colour, warm     0.28-0.30     0.28-0.37     0.0078

**And that measurement corrects the plain saturation statistic**, which described the green frames wrongly: a
monochrome-green picture has a **large** channel spread and a **small** hue spread (0.06-0.18 against a colour
photograph's 0.3661), where `(max-min)/max` reports 0.55 for both. So the operation to model is **a gain on one axis,
not a desaturation** -- which is what a monochrome viewfinder fed to a colour recorder produces.

**`atsim/codecs/nightvision.py`** is the physical layer, and every term is a mechanism rather than a grade:

  * **`Illuminator`** -- an inverse-square lamp at the camera inside a cone, so the near field is lit and a corridor
    recedes to black. **Its distance mapping was inverted on the first attempt**: measured, `illuminator=1.0` gave near
    2.78, **mid 16.67**, far 0.49 -- the peak in the middle of the frame, which is not a falloff. Corrected, the field
    now runs **7.41 at the near field to 0.11 at the horizon, 58 of 58 steps non-increasing.**
  * **`IR_ALBEDO`** -- near-infrared reflectance per material class, **skin 1.55, foliage 2.10, white fabric 1.80,
    dark clothing 0.28, asphalt 0.32**. This is the mechanism that makes a night-vision picture recognisable and it
    **cannot be derived from a visible image** -- the two albedos are independent -- so the module states that the
    estimate is deliberately conservative (luminance to a low power) rather than claiming a spectral measurement the
    input cannot support.
  * **`add_illuminator`** -- the lamp's light returned by the scene, with the lamp's own faint visible leak as a
    **near-field violet cast that is absent in the far field**, reproduced by tinting the lamp rather than the picture.
  * **`monochrome_green`** -- one luminance carried on the green axis in the clip's own proportions.
    **Its polarity was inverted on the first attempt**: `green_mix=0.0` returned the colour picture (ratio
    0.329:0.388:0.284) while the parameter's name and its own comment said 0 was the pure green view. Corrected, and the
    measured ratio is now **0.317:0.410:0.273** against the clip's **0.29:0.46:0.25**.
  * **`accumulate`** -- frame summation, and **it is verified against theory**: on synthetic noise the high-frequency
    energy falls by a factor of **2.878 over 8 frames against sqrt(8) = 2.83**, which is the physical distinction
    between Super NightShot and a single long exposure. **That is the one part of this work so far whose correctness is
    established by an independent prediction rather than by a target.**

**Two faults were found and fixed in the module's own first version**, both of the kind this project keeps meeting: the
illuminator's divergence at short distance, and a parameter whose behaviour contradicted its name.

**Honest state**: the physical layer exists, is inert at mode 0, and matches the reference on the two quantities measured
so far. **It is not yet wired into the camera stage, the schema, or the WebUI**, so nothing is visible in the renderer
yet, and the green gain needs a final adjustment (**0.410 against a target of 0.46**). The next steps are the schedule
in the todo list: camera wiring, the three modes' schema parameters, then the WebUI, then per-mode calibration against
the segmented reference.

make frame accumulation work on the real render path, and fix two faults that were hiding it

**Accumulation now runs, once, at the sensor stage -- and three faults had to be found to get there.**

**The first: it never ran at all.** `_nv_history` was a list on the stage, and each frame is rendered by its own
`_render_index` call, so the list only ever held the current frame and `len(history) == n` was never reached for n > 1.
**Measured on the real path, 1, 4 and 8 frames gave identical noise (0.00994)** -- which is what an accumulator
receiving one frame looks like. The earlier test passed only because it built a sequence by hand and rendered a single
frame of it. Frames now come from the **source**, which the stage can reach through `ctx.source`.

**The second: the index was read from the wrong object, silently.** The helper used
`getattr(self.ctx, "index", 0)`, and `RenderContext` has **no `index`** -- so it used 0 every time, the window clipped to
one frame, and accumulation again did nothing. **A missing attribute that yields a plausible value instead of raising is
how a mechanism ends up inert while looking correctly wired**, so the index is now a required parameter, taken from
`FrameBuffer.index` where it actually lives.

**The third, and the one that took longest: the mechanism was wired in two places, and the second one destroyed the
picture.** A display-stage block survived from the first attempt and ran *after* the scene-stage version had done the
work, **overwriting the fully-processed picture with the mean of several source frames** -- 0.1768 replacing 0.45429.
The stage trace is what found it:

    _night_vision scene     0.17680 -> 0.45429
    _roll_off               0.45429 -> 0.46757
    _night_vision display   0.17680 -> 0.20216    <- a SOURCE-level mean, not the processed picture

**A mechanism wired in two places is worse than one wired in none: the second silently wins and the first's
measurements describe a picture that never reaches the output.**

**And the green view had a 15.6% luminance gain from a constant that was never checked.** Measured on flat fields:
0.20 -> 0.23114, 0.40 -> 0.46228, 0.70 -> 0.80898, all at a gain of **1.15569**. The formula was
`lum * [0.29, 0.46, 0.25] * 3.0`, and those proportions **sum to exactly 1.0**, so the `* 3.0` was the whole of the
gain -- **a monochrome view does not brighten the scene it displays.** Normalising by the weights' own luma sum leaves
the hue exactly as the clip shows it and makes the measured gain **1.0000** on every level below clipping.

**The verification is the static-source control**, which is the one case whose answer is known: averaging eight
*identical* frames must return that frame's brightness unchanged.

    mode                      MOVING source            STATIC source (the control)
    NightShot, 1 frame        mean 0.4255  hf 0.00967   mean 0.4249  hf 0.01010
    Super NightShot, 8 frames mean 0.4351  hf 0.00297   mean 0.4252  hf 0.00724

**The static control holds its brightness to within 0.1%** (0.4249 against 0.4252), and **the noise falls by 3.26x on
the moving source against the 2.83 of sqrt(8)** -- the physical signature of accumulation, now on the real render path
rather than on a hand-built sequence.

**The three modes, measured against the clip's segmented targets:**

    mode                          mean     blown     G share   hue       hf        ratio
    off (baseline)                0.1203   0.00794   0.2931    0.5326    0.00795   0.580:0.293:0.127
    **1 NightShot**               0.4246   **0.00000** **0.5445** **0.0832** 0.01007 0.278:0.544:0.178
    **2 Super NightShot, 8 fr**   0.4339   0.00000   0.5415    0.0820   **0.00463** 0.280:0.541:0.179
    **3 Color Slow Shutter, 8 fr** 0.4563  0.10631   0.3367    0.3896    0.00547   0.385:0.337:0.278

    the clip's green segments:  G share 0.44-0.58,  hue 0.06-0.18,  ratio 0.29:0.46:0.25

**NightShot now lands inside the clip's green range on both quantities** (G share 0.5445, hue 0.0832) with **no blown
pixels at all**, and **Super NightShot's noise is less than half of NightShot's** (0.00463 against 0.01007).
**Color Slow Shutter keeps its colour** (G share 0.3367 against NightShot's 0.5445), which is what the mode is.

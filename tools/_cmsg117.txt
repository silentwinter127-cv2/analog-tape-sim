wire the three night-vision parameters that were declared but never read, and verify the noise law

**The check that found them, and it is the reason they were found.** Each was varied over its **full declared range**
and the frames compared. Three produced **bit-identical output**:

    nv_green_gain     0.5 to 1.6     identical frame
    nv_read_noise     0.0 to 0.2     identical frame
    nv_ir_response    0.0            identical frame

**All three are the "declared, reachable, and never read" fault** -- the same one this project has found in `agc_range`,
`chroma_under_luma`, `player.line_delay`, `tape.row_chroma_*`, `player.chroma_vert_blend`, `crt_age`, `convergence`,
`mask_depth`, and in `nv_*` itself before it reached the schema. **Reading the code would have shown the fields
declared and unreferenced, which is suggestive; varying them and comparing frames is what makes it a fact.**

**And `nv_ir_response` was the worst of the three**, because it is the parameter that means *the IR-cut filter has been
removed* -- the entire physical premise of NightShot -- and setting it to 0 changed nothing at all.

**What each now does, and the placement that carries meaning:**

  * **`nv_green_gain`** scales the green channel in the monochrome view, then the weights are **re-normalised** so the
    change is a hue shift and not a brightness change. Measured: 0.5 gives a G share of **0.2891**, 1.6 gives
    **0.7520**, and the mean moves by under 0.05 rather than in proportion;
  * **`nv_ir_response`** gates the whole near-infrared term. At 0 the mean falls from **0.42520 to 0.19862**, i.e. back
    towards the no-night-vision baseline, which is what "the filter is in place" means;
  * **`nv_read_noise`** adds the sensor's read noise **to each frame before the sum**. Read noise adds per frame at the
    sensor, so N frames add it N times in quadrature while the signal adds N times coherently and the ratio improves as
    `sqrt(N)`. **Applied after the sum it would not fall at all, and the model could not show why the camera has an
    accumulating mode** -- which is the entire content of Super NightShot. So the placement is not a detail.

**And that law is now verified on the real render path**, not on a hand-built sequence:

    mode                           mean       hf (noise)   1/sqrt(N)   measured ratio
    NightShot (1 frame)            0.42801    0.01030      1.0000      1.0000
    Super NightShot, 2 frames      0.43158    0.00775      0.7071      0.7527
    Super NightShot, 4 frames      0.43656    0.00535      0.5000      0.5193
    Super NightShot, 8 frames      0.44113    0.00340      0.3536      0.3304
    Super NightShot, 16 frames     0.44541    0.00240      0.2500      **0.2326**

**Sixteen frames measure 0.2326 against the predicted 0.2500**, and the ratio falls monotonically with N. A reading of
1.00 for every N would have meant the noise was being added after the sum; it is not.

**Also corrected in the schema**: `nv_mode` defaulted to 0 and `nv_accumulate` to 1, so selecting a mode did nothing
until two more values were set -- not a usable default for a named function. They are now **1** and **8**, and the
`nv_mode` label names all four functions (**0关/1NightShot/2Super/3ColorSlow**) because `ParamSpec` has no `choices`
field, so the selector cannot carry names through the parameter machinery. A proper dropdown would need `app.js`
changes and is left as its own step.

**Where the objective stands.** The physics, the module, the camera wiring, the schema, the render path, the
calibration and the parameters are all done and measured. **What remains is the WebUI step**: the twelve parameters
already appear in the interface automatically (they are in the camera layer's "光学与传感器" group, confirmed by reading

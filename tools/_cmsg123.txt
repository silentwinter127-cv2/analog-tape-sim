calibrate the colour models to the footage, and measure what the dynamic can and cannot represent

**1. The green display's proportions were the corridor's, not the camera's.**

The model rendered `0.29:0.46:0.25` because that is what an earlier NightShot reference measured -- a **corridor, with
no coloured emitters**. This clip's stable green stretch -- 874 frames, **29.1 s**, at the source's 30 fps -- measures:

    G share     p10 0.4044   p50 0.4283   p90 0.4567
    R share     p10 0.1880   p50 0.2162   p90 0.2456
    B share     p10 0.3460   p50 0.3528   p90 0.3637
    hue spread  p10 0.1552   p50 0.2281   p90 0.2891
    median ratio   0.2162 : 0.4283 : 0.3528

**Less red, less green, and 0.10 more blue** -- because the subject is an **RGB case** whose own emitters are blue, cyan
and magenta, and the mode passes a residue of their chroma. **A fixed triple cannot fit both subjects**, and the honest
reading is that the triple describes *the display plus what the sensor passed*, not the display alone. So it is now the
measured triple, and the result lands on it exactly:

    target (measured)   0.2168  0.4295  0.3538
    the model now       0.2168  0.4294  0.3538
    difference         +0.0000 -0.0001 +0.0000

**And the luminance normalisation is preserved**, which is the property a previous round established by measurement -- a
constant multiplier had been giving a **15.6% gain** (1.15569 on every flat field) and the normalisation brought it to
1.0000. Re-measured after this change: **1.00000 on flat fields of 0.2, 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8.**

**The hue spread is a limit this exposed rather than fixed.** At `green_mix = 1.0` the model collapses the hue to
**0.0003**, while the clip reads **0.1552-0.2891**. A real NightShot frame is not perfectly monochrome, because the
picture still contains saturated emitters the sensor passes. The weights now match the clip's *balance*; the *residue*
of chroma is a separate property and is **not** reproduced -- recorded rather than papered over.

**2. The dynamic: one part was measurable and corrected, the other is a different kind of motion.**

The green stretch carries a **1.60 s periodicity in motion at r = 0.671** and **1.77 s in luma at r = 0.515**, with
**85% of the frame-to-frame change being structured rather than a level shift** -- so it is movement, not gain.

**The rates were wrong and are corrected.** The model's dominant component was `postural sway` at 0.9 Hz, and its
composite offset autocorrelated at **25 frames (0.83 s) at r = 0.15** -- the footage's *second harmonic* rather than its
fundamental. Scaling every rate by **0.60** (to 0.54 / 1.26 / 3.30 / 5.70 Hz) moves the composite to the footage's
timescale.

**But the strength is not reachable and the sweep says why.** Across **fifteen combinations** of rate and drift the
autocorrelation peak stays between **0.12 and 0.22** against the footage's **0.671** -- and lowering the drift does not
raise it, it makes a drift component dominant and pushes the period out to **4-9 s**. Re-measured across three seeds
after the correction, the period is **42, 45, 100, 47 and 128 frames**: **there is no stable period**, which is exactly
what r ≈ 0.15 means.

**So this model represents an *unsteady hand* -- random motion at a characteristic rate -- and the footage shows an
*operator sweeping deliberately*, which is a different kind of motion.** A second sway term was **not** added: that would
give the model two sources for one behaviour, the fault this project has met repeatedly. The rates are corrected, the
strength is recorded at the profile in the source, and the amplitude is unchanged in kind so the shake still reads as a
hand rather than an oscillator (dx std 5.456 px).

**3. And the scan itself is now reusable.** All **3111 frames at the source's 30 fps** were decoded through a pipe at
480x360 and their statistics **cached to `tools/calib/scan30.json`**, so further analysis needs no re-decoding. **Every
frame carries a real picture** -- mean luma bottoms out at 0.0606, not at black -- which corrects the earlier 2 fps
reading, where the "empty frames" were an artefact of measuring on 1440x1080 PNGs.

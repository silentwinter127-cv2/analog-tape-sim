render the chroma residue so it can be judged by eye, then derive the exposure loop's damping

**1. The residue, rendered -- and the first metric I read it by was the wrong one.**

Three panels through the whole chain on the case photograph, the same subject the three NightShot segments were shot
of, with the defects off so the colour is what is being compared:

    residue 0.00   R:G:B 0.1923:0.4480:0.3597   the rigid projection
    residue 0.35   R:G:B 0.1931:0.4385:0.3684   the calibration
    residue 1.00   R:G:B 0.1960:0.4220:0.3820   chroma untouched

**By eye the three are clearly different** -- the left is an overwhelming green in which the case's RGB lighting has no
colour at all, the middle is still unmistakably a green night-vision view with the blue-cyan axis coming in, and the
right has the lighting's colour back and reads as blue rather than green. **The means are 0.4949, 0.4950 and 0.4951**,
so brightness is carried on finished frames as well as on flat fields.

**But the red share barely moves (0.1923 to 0.1960) and that made the parameter look nearly inert.** It is not:

    the photograph's own chroma, per channel      +0.0204  -0.0312  +0.1070     **blue-biased**
    the display's mean absolute chroma, residue 0  [0.0964  0.0495  0.0024]
    the display's mean absolute chroma, residue 1  [0.0964  0.0309  0.1122]     blue up 47x
    and the output's channel shares move through the green-blue axis: G 0.4294 -> 0.3362, B 0.3538 -> 0.4466

**So the residue is working, and it moves the blue-green axis rather than the red one -- because this subject's chroma
is blue-biased.** **Every verification of this parameter had used the red share alone, which is blind to the axis this
photograph actually occupies** -- the same fault as reading the wrong statistic, in the same round I had just fixed one.

**And the caveat is repeated because it decides how much the images settle**: the three segments were all shot **in the
mode**, so their colour source material does not exist. The residue is calibrated so the display **follows the subject
at all** (a fixed output ratio for every input became a spread of 1.20), not to reproduce the measured 1.44.

**2. The exposure loop's damping, derived from the measured overshoot.**

The loop is **first-order** -- one time constant, `tau_s = 1.1` -- and **a first-order lag cannot overshoot at all**,
while the footage overshoots by **28.60% to 81.51%** of the level against a **2.65-6.22%** noise floor. **So the loop
is the wrong order, not merely mistuned.** Inverting `OS = exp(-pi*zeta/sqrt(1-zeta^2))` gives:

    NIGHTSHOT 1 -> NIGHTSHOT 2   28.6%   zeta 0.370
    NightShot -> normal          38.4%   zeta 0.292
    ? -> NIGHTSHOT 1             44.0%   zeta 0.253
    NightShot -> ?               49.0%   zeta 0.221
    NIGHTSHOT 2 -> after         81.5%   zeta 0.065

**And a claim was written ahead of its evidence and is withdrawn.** The first version of this script asserted the five
agreed "to within a factor of two" **before computing them**; the measured spread is **a factor of 5.7**. So the number
reported is the **median, 0.253**, with the spread stated -- and the five are **ordered** (28.6% up to 81.5%), which is
a **pattern rather than scatter**: the transitions are not all the same kind of event, and a single damping cannot
reproduce that. Saying so is more useful than averaging it away.

**The fitted response reproduces the median exactly** -- 43.97% overshoot, first peak at 3.57 s, 63% at 1.50 s against
the first-order 1.10 s -- so changing the order barely moves the settling time while making the overshoot possible.

**And a disagreement is stated rather than smoothed over**: `tau_s = 1.1 s` with `zeta = 0.253` gives an underdamped
period of **7.14 s**, while the luma was measured to oscillate at **1.77 s** (r = 0.515). **The 1.77 s came from the
green stretch's own luma, which includes the operator panning the camera, so it bounds the loop's period rather than
measuring it.** The overshoot is what the seven transitions constrain, each against its own before-and-after levels, so
`zeta` comes from it and the period stays at the loop's existing constant.

**No code changed this round.** Both items were measurements and both produced a number; the damping's implementation is
the next step, and the fitted `zeta` is stated so it can be applied without re-deriving it.

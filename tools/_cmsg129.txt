**1. The residue: 0.35 was right and my reason for raising it was wrong.**

I raised it to 0.50 because the model's **red and green** chroma read 2.5x the real NIGHTSHOT 0's. The distance to that
frame says the opposite, and monotonically:

    the real NIGHTSHOT 0    [0.0480  0.0245  0.0213]
    residue 0.35            [0.1068  0.0544  0.0091]    distance 0.0336   **closest**
    residue 0.50            [0.1137  0.0579  0.0123]    distance 0.0360
    residue 0.70            [0.1229  0.0625  0.0166]    distance 0.0392
    residue 0.90            [0.1320  0.0670  0.0210]    distance 0.0423   **monotonically worse**

**Raising it moves the model further from the real frame**, and the reason is that the mismatch is in the **channel
proportions**: the real frame's channels span a factor of **2.26** and the model's span **11.7**, with red and green
always too high and blue always too low. **A residue scales the chroma the picture already has; it cannot rebalance it.**
So 0.35 is restored and the refutation is recorded beside it. **And I had again read only part of the channels -- red and
green -- in the same round in which I had just written that reading one channel was the mistake.**

**Per-mode residues are set where the measurement supports it**: NIGHTSHOT 0 is distinct, and **1 and 2 share a value
because the measurement cannot separate them** (the chroma moves 9% from 1 to 2, inside the frame-to-frame variation of a
handheld shot). Writing three values would repeat the earlier mistake of turning a visible difference between two takes
into a mechanism it does not support.

**2. The colour noise exists and cannot come from the existing term.**

Measured in the shadows of the three frames, the per-channel high-frequency energy is **0.00736 / 0.00659 / 0.00836** --
a **27% spread**, so the noise is coloured. **`nv_read_noise` is a single scalar, which produces luminance noise only,
so it cannot produce this however large it is set** -- a missing property rather than a mistuned one. `nv_chroma_noise`
is added: each channel gets its own draw, **the mean across the channels is removed** so the term adds colour without
changing the noise's contribution to brightness, and it is **a fraction of `read_noise`** rather than a second source.

**Verified as far as it goes**: with `read_noise` at zero there is **no noise at all** whatever `chroma_noise` says
(0.00000 in every channel), and the noise magnitude rises with the parameter (hf 0.0557 at chroma 0, 0.0723 at chroma 1)
so the term is neither inert nor a second noise source.

**3. And the channel spread it is meant to produce could not be confirmed, which is reported rather than papered over.**

The target is a **1.27** spread between channels. What the measurement gives, at every scale tried and on independent
realisations:

    chroma 0.00   spread 1.002 to 1.013 across block sizes 1, 2, 4, 8, 16
    chroma 0.27   spread 1.003 to 1.016
    chroma 1.00   spread 1.002 to 1.015

**So the spread is 1.00-1.02 where it should be near 1.27, and it does not grow with the parameter.** Two explanations
remain and **I could not distinguish them**: the estimator (a mean absolute difference over a large field, which
converges) may be blind to a per-channel variance difference, or the per-channel draws may not be independent as
intended. **Recording it as unresolved is the honest state** -- and the pattern is by now familiar: the parameter is
wired, reachable and moves the picture, while the statistic chosen to verify it cannot see the property it was chosen
for. **The next step is an estimator with a known answer on synthetic per-channel noise**, not another sweep.

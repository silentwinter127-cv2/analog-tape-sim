**What was attempted and why it was abandoned.**  Chasing the chain's brightness mismatch led to the green display's
weights, whose normalisation guarantees `weights @ LUMA == 1` while a picture's brightness is the **sum** of the three
channels.  Replacing that division with a sum division was tried and **broke the display outright** -- a grey input came
out at **0.33333** of its brightness, identically on every input.

**The constant was the diagnostic and I still could not converge.**  A property that fails by the *same factor on every
input* is a scaling fault rather than a colour one, and the function was found to contain the normalisation **more than
once** -- a duplicate removed, another still present, and **after five attempts I could not state with confidence what
the function contained**.  **A mechanism applied twice, with a surviving copy, is the fault shape this project has met
repeatedly**, and the honest response when the instrument cannot be trusted is to stop rather than to keep editing.
**`nightvision.py` was restored to the last verified commit (8d8b42f)**, and **163 checks pass**.

**And the measurement that motivated all of it does not support the explanation I was working from.**  On the restored
function the **luma gain is exactly 1.00000 on every input** -- grey, blue-ish, strongly green and the photograph's own
-- which is a genuine property.  What varies is the **sum** gain: 0.93315 grey, 0.93615 blue-ish, 1.09331 strongly
green, **0.86499 for the photograph's own colour**.  **So the display is luminance-preserving and its sum gain is below
1 on this subject**, which means it **cannot** account for the model reading **0.4443 against the real frame's 0.2349**.
**I had inferred the cause from the 1.89 factor and a plausible mechanism, without checking their signs against each
other** -- the sum gain is *less* than one, so it moves the picture the wrong way to explain a value that is too high.

**So the brightness mismatch remains open and its cause is elsewhere in the chain.**  The candidates are the exposure
and illuminator terms and the mode's own gain, none of which this round examined.  **The value of the round is the
measurement, not a change**: the display carries luminance exactly, and the sum/luminance distinction matters only at
the 0.87-1.09 level, which is a **0-13% effect** rather than the 89% that needs explaining.

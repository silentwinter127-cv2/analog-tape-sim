**What is fixed and verified.**

**The mode presets no longer override the lamp.**  Spying on the argument the stage actually passes showed
`nv_illuminator` set to 0.00, 0.15 and 0.25 **all arriving at `add_illuminator` as 0.15** -- the parameter never reached
the function that uses it, because `mode_spec` rewrote it from the preset table.  Both NightShot entries are cleaned and
**`mode_spec` now passes 0.000 / 0.100 / 0.250 through unchanged**, verified.  `colour_slow_shutter` keeps
`illuminator = 0.0`, where *lamp off* is what defines the mode rather than a value imposed on it.

**And the rule it violated was already in that file, written in this session**: *"a preset that overrides a physical
control is a preset that removes a control."*  It had been applied to `ir_albedo_mix` and `ir_response` **while the
lamp's own strength -- the one control a NightShot user most needs -- was left in**.  A second occurrence was missed by
the first edit because the `super_nightshot` entry carries `chroma_residue=0.90` while the anchor assumed 0.35: **an
anchor derived from one entry does not match its sibling**, which is why that edit reported success while the chain
still read flat.

**What was attempted, measured, and reverted.**

The field's **clamp** was identified as a real fault: `min(ref^2/d^2, near_bound)` with `ref = 0.75` and
`near_bound = 3.0` reaches **562500 at the beam's centre**, so the clamp flattened the whole near field into a
**plateau**, and the peak-normalisation added afterwards normalised **the plateau rather than a beam centre**.  It was
rebuilt as a smooth inverse-square falloff from a lamp at the lens -- `L^2 / (L^2 + r^2)` normalised at the aim -- and
**the field itself is now correct**: measured, it has a **smooth peak** (0.0075% of pixels at the maximum), is **1.0000
at the corner against 1.2094 at the centre**, and stays radially symmetric with the vertical tilt set by `beam_y` rather
than baked in.

**But the chain's level went from a flat 0.3888 to a flat 0.8198** -- still **identical at every setting from 0.02 to
0.40** -- and `add_illuminator` at 0.15 should contribute about **0.18** by the field's own magnitude, while the render
gained **0.62**.  **So a second, larger gain is being applied somewhere between the field and the finished picture, and
I could not locate it before running out of room to work safely.**

**So the field change is reverted** and `nightvision.py` is restored to the last committed state, which keeps the
verified preset fix and leaves the level where it was.  **Reverting rather than shipping a half-traced change is the
same decision as the earlier `monochrome_green` revert**, and for the same reason: a wrong value in the model is worse
than a known-bad one that is documented.

**163 checks pass, selftest clean, working tree clean.**

**The two measurements that make the next attempt cheap**, both already established and neither needing to be redone:

  * **the control must move smoothly and monotonically with `nv_illuminator`** -- the check is that the level is neither
    flat nor step-like, and the current state fails it (flat from 0.02 up), so the fault is unambiguous;
  * **and the field's magnitude must stay within the picture's range**: the real NIGHTSHOT 0 is **1.19x** its own source
    (0.2349 against 0.1973), so **everything the mode adds -- lamp, IR response and display together -- is 0.0376 of
    luma**.  A lamp contributing 0.62 is more than sixteen times that, which is the quantity to check rather than the
    field's internal profile.

**Where the second gain most likely sits, stated as a suspicion rather than a finding**: between `illuminator_field` and
the finished picture, the candidates are `add_illuminator`'s own response and tint terms and the interaction with
`ir_highlights` -- and **the way to settle it is the attribution method that worked twice in this session** (switch each
term off in turn and record the level), not another parameter sweep.

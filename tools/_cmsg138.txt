fix the contrast compression: `cross_luma` was scaled eight times too large

**The result.**  A black-to-white ramp through the chain now comes out at **1.188 of its own contrast**, where it came
out at **0.800** before -- so the **38% loss is gone**.  It now slightly *exceeds* unity rather than falling well short,
so the acceptance test (near 1.0) is **not yet met** and the remaining 19% is the next item.

**The measurement that named it, taken inside the real function.**  Two attempts to locate this by *reproducing* the
arithmetic both gave figures the code does not produce, so a gated probe was added inside `_separate` itself.  On a ramp:

    yc_separate #1                     0.31227    1.078 of the ramp
    after horizontal_shift             0.31201
    the round trip's composite         0.31203
    yc_separate #2                     0.37654    1.300
    after the chroma rescale            0.37654
    after cross_color                   0.37654    (chroma only -- correct)
    the residue's own spread            1.05817    **2.8x the luma itself**
    the correction at 0.15              0.16239    **43% of the luma**
    after cross_luma                    0.23234    **38% of the luma subtracted**
    RETURN                              0.23234

**So `y_band = y_band + cross_luma * 0.5 * resid * mod` was adding a quantity 43% the size of the luma, out of phase.**
The residue is `chroma_band - remod_chroma(i, q)`, and **both of its terms carry the whole signal** -- the band as the
split produced it and the re-modulation as this code built it -- so their difference is not the small crosstalk term the
comment describes but a large quantity dominated by how the two differ.  **What the term models is the moving dot
pattern, which is a few percent of a picture, not half of it**, so the scale is set to put the correction at about **5%
of the luma's spread** for `cross_luma` at its default.

**And the earlier "cross_luma = 0 changes nothing" reading was measured in the wrong place**: it read the chain's *final
output*, where the term's effect is absorbed, rather than `_separate`'s *return value*, where it is 38%.  **That is the
third time this session a parameter has been called inert because it was measured at a point where its effect does not
appear.**

**Two things are recorded as still open, both measured rather than guessed:**

  * **the second split's own gain.**  `yc_separate` with `comb_depth=0.55` returns a column-constant picture's luma at
    **1.206** of its input, and over the depth's range it goes from **0.272 to 5.456** -- where a separator that divides
    the spectrum should return **1.000** at every setting.  **And `vert_blend` changes nothing at either value.**  A
    correct separator cannot have a depth-dependent gain on a signal with no vertical detail, so this is a formula
    fault rather than a calibration;
  * **and `comb_depth` does not reach the chain.**  Swept from 0.00 to 0.85, the chain's output is **bit-identical**
    (1.188 ramp, 1.322 split) -- while the isolated call to `yc_separate` with the same values moves the luma by a
    factor of twenty.  **A parameter that works in isolation and not through the chain is the fault class this project
    records most often**, and it is why no setting of it can close the remaining 19%.

**The probe is gated by `ATSIM_SEP_PROBE` and verified inert without it** -- a render with the variable unset prints
nothing -- **so it can be used again without editing the source, which is what the two failed reconstructions cost.**

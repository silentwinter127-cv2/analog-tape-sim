fix the inversion: six of the player's parameters were being read from the FORMAT layer

**The fault, printed from the stage as it is built.**  `PlayerStage.__init__` takes two dictionaries:

    p = self.cfg_of("player")
    f = self.cfg_of("format")

**and reads six of its own parameters out of `f`** -- the format layer's:

    self.comb_depth       = float(f.get("comb_depth", 0.55))
    self.cross_color      = float(f.get("cross_color", 0.45))
    self.cross_luma       = float(f.get("cross_luma", 0.30))
    self.burst_error_deg  = float(f.get("burst_phase_error", 5.0))
    self.chroma_bw        = float(f.get("chroma_bandwidth", 0.75)) * 1e6
    self.luma_bw_hz       = float(f.get("luma_bandwidth", 3.0)) * 1e6

**so the interface's `player.*` values for those six did nothing at all.**  Measured directly: **the player's chroma
bandwidth read 2.000 MHz for every value requested**, including 0.5 and 3.0, and a sweep across those values gave
bit-identical output.

**And that was the inversion.**  The stage's second split uses that bandwidth, and on the real photograph it takes
**0.770 of the composite** as chroma at 2 MHz; the comb's `1/(1-0.55)` multiplies that by **2.143**, so the chroma band
ends up carrying **1.099 of the composite** -- and `luma = comp - chroma` goes **negative** where the picture is bright.

**Measured, with the layer fixed and the schema's own 0.75 MHz:**

    player chroma_bw   polarity    contrast
    0.50 MHz           +0.935      0.1928
    0.75 MHz           +0.935      0.1928      <- the schema's default
    1.00 MHz           +0.935      0.1928
    2.00 MHz           -0.358      0.1678      <- inverted
    3.00 MHz           -0.358      0.1678

**So the picture is no longer inverted at the default, and the contrast is 0.95x the footage's rather than the 0.64x it
has been throughout this investigation.**  On the rendered result the polarity is **+0.844** against the source, where
it was **-0.358**.

**Why six corrections to the chroma division all failed.**  Every one of them edited the division's formula, and the
division was never the fault: **the band handed to it was too wide**, because the width came from the wrong layer.  Two
of those attempts repaired the polarity while destroying the contrast and the rest fixed neither, which is what a fix
applied one stage away from the fault looks like.  **All six were reverted and are recorded as failures.**

**And a correction to an earlier round's conclusion**: I had attributed the inversion to the per-row timebase
displacement breaking the subcarrier's line-to-line inversion.  **Reading the code refutes it** -- the shift is applied to
**baseband** `i` and `q`, and the carrier is generated afterwards by `remod_chroma`, so a baseband shift does not rotate
carrier phase.  That mechanism was inferred from a number plus a reading rather than measured, which is the same mistake
in a new place.

**What remains open**: with the polarity correct the contrast is **0.68x** of the footage's on the full chain
(0.1373 against 0.2032), and the mode itself accounts for most of that -- measured earlier as the chain costing 11.4%
and the night-vision scene stage a further 22.5%.  That is the next item, and it is a different fault from this one.

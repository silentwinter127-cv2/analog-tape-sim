fix the inversion: six of the player's own parameters were read from the FORMAT layer

**The fault, printed from the stage as it is built.**  `PlayerStage.__init__` takes two dictionaries:

    p = self.cfg_of("player")
    f = self.cfg_of("format")

**and reads six of its own parameters out of `f`** -- the format layer's -- so the interface's `player.*` values for
them did nothing at all:

    self.comb_depth       = float(f.get("comb_depth", 0.55))
    self.cross_color      = float(f.get("cross_color", 0.45))
    self.cross_luma       = float(f.get("cross_luma", 0.30))
    self.burst_error_deg  = float(f.get("burst_phase_error", 5.0))
    self.chroma_bw        = float(f.get("chroma_bandwidth", 0.75)) * 1e6
    self.luma_bw_hz       = float(f.get("luma_bandwidth", 3.0)) * 1e6

**Measured: the player's chroma bandwidth read 2.000 MHz for every value requested**, including 0.5 and 3.0, and a sweep
across those values gave bit-identical output.

**And that was the inversion.**  At 2 MHz the stage's second split takes **0.770 of the composite** as chroma, and the
comb's `1/(1-0.55)` multiplies that by **2.143**, so the chroma band carries **1.099 of the composite** -- and
`luma = comp - chroma` goes **negative** where the picture is bright.  With the layer fixed and the schema's own
0.75 MHz:

    player chroma_bw   polarity    contrast
    0.50 MHz           +0.935      0.1928
    0.75 MHz           +0.935      0.1928      <- the schema's default
    1.00 MHz           +0.935      0.1928
    2.00 MHz           -0.358      0.1678      <- inverted
    3.00 MHz           -0.358      0.1678

**So the picture is no longer inverted at the default.**  On the three-mode test the polarity is **+0.844 to +0.852**
against the source, where it was **-0.358**.

**Why six corrections to the chroma division all failed.**  Every one edited the division's formula, and the division was
never the fault: **the band handed to it was too wide**, because the width came from the wrong layer.  Two of those
attempts repaired the polarity while destroying the contrast and the rest fixed neither -- which is what a fix applied
one stage away from the fault looks like.  **All six were reverted and are recorded as failures.**

**Two corrections to earlier conclusions, both from reading the code rather than inferring from numbers:**

  * I had attributed the inversion to the per-row timebase displacement breaking the subcarrier's line-to-line
    inversion.  **Refuted**: the shift is applied to **baseband** `i` and `q`, and the carrier is generated afterwards by
    `remod_chroma`, so a baseband shift does not rotate carrier phase;
  * and the "`fft_bandpass` takes more than the composite holds" reading came from a signal that was not the one the
    stage receives.  On a clean composite the split is correct -- a column-constant picture returns chroma at
    **0.00009** against an input of **0.28958**, luma at **1.000**.

**Recorded as attempted and NOT working**: a variance-preserving normalisation on `ir_highlights`, which is the term
measured to cost the night-vision mode about a quarter of its contrast (`ir_highlights` alone takes a photograph from
0.1894 to 0.1308, i.e. 0.691x, while `add_illuminator` measures 1.043x and the green display 0.999x).  The normalisation
is in place on the blended luminance, and **the chain's figures improved** -- mode off 0.94x the footage's contrast and
mode on 0.68x, against 0.64x before -- **but the term measured on its own is unchanged at 0.691x**, because a
mean-preserving rescale at the end of that function pulls the spread back.  **It is not claimed as fixed.**

163 checks pass, selftest clean.

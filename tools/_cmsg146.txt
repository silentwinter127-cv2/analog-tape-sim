add the shot noise term `note.txt` asks for, and measure that it is right in kind and too small in degree

**What the note asks for, and what the model had.**

    "加入随曝光和信号强度变化的噪声、暗部读出噪声、固定图样噪声，以及高亮区域的饱和。
     不要简单地对整张图叠加均匀颗粒；暗部和高光的表现应该不同。"

**The model had read noise only** -- one draw, `rng.normal(0, read_noise)`, **the same absolute amount whatever the
signal**.  A real sensor has two components with **opposite** behaviour:

  * **read noise** is a fixed absolute amount, so it is *relatively* largest in the shadows -- which is why a
    night-vision frame is grainy in the dark and clean on a lit surface.  The model already had this;
  * **shot noise** is the statistical variation in photon arrival, whose **standard deviation grows as the square root
    of the signal**, so in absolute terms it is largest in the highlights and above some level it overtakes read noise.

**A model with only one of them is wrong at one end of the tonal range whatever its total noise measures** -- which is
why a single scalar could not have found this, and why `note.txt`'s phrasing is about the two ends differing rather than
about the noise being larger.

**So `shot_noise` is a separate field and a separate term**: one field scaled per pixel by `sqrt(lum)`, which is what a
Poisson arrival gives (variance proportional to the mean, so the deviation goes as its square root), tied to the signal
exactly rather than on average.

**And the prediction was stated so it could fail**, then measured with the noise isolated temporally -- several seeds on a
static subject, so anything varying between renders is noise and nothing else:

    read 0.00  shot 0.00     dark 0.00983   bright 0.03496   ratio 0.281
    read 0.10  shot 0.00     dark 0.01269   bright 0.03581   ratio 0.354   **+0.073**
    read 0.25  shot 0.00     dark 0.01702   bright 0.03803   ratio 0.448   **+0.166**
    read 0.00  shot 0.10     dark 0.01009   bright 0.03585   ratio 0.281   +0.000
    read 0.00  shot 0.25     dark 0.01095   bright 0.03911   ratio 0.280   **-0.001**

**The two move the ratio in opposite directions, which is the physical claim**, and raising read noise raises it while
raising shot noise slightly lowers it.  **But the shot term's effect is 0.001 where the read term's is 0.166** -- two
orders of magnitude smaller than the term it is supposed to balance.

**So it is right in kind and far too weak in degree, and that is what is recorded rather than a claim that the
mechanism is modelled.**  Two candidate reasons, both testable and neither tested:

  * **it is applied to luminance only**, as one field across the three channels, so a per-channel draw would make it
    larger in the same way `chroma_noise` is a separate term from `read_noise`;
  * **and the picture's own structure dominates the statistic**: the bright third of this frame carries real detail whose
    residual is larger than any noise added, so a term that is physically correct can still barely move a 3x3-residual
    measure.  **The footage's own figure is 0.330 with the same confound**, so the statistic cannot distinguish the two
    explanations -- a synthetic flat field would.

**What is verified**: the default path is unchanged with `shot_noise = 0`, the parameter is read, the term runs by
default at 0.10, and **163 checks pass with selftest clean**.

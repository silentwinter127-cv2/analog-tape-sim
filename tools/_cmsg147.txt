**The correction, which matters more than anything else here.**  Last round I reported that `accumulate` was
**inert** -- "temporal noise at `accumulate = 8` was 0.07968 against a single frame's 0.07968, no reduction at all".
**That was wrong, and the mistake was mine**: the test set `nv_mode = 1` *and* `nv_accumulate = 8`, and `mode_spec`
builds the spec from the **mode's preset** on every frame -- so mode 1 forced `accumulate` back to 1 and the term never
ran.  **The parameter is an override; the mode is the control.**  Re-measured with the mode named, and with an ensemble
over frame indices (which for a flickering source is an ensemble over noise realisations):

    mode 1 (nightshot, one frame)        0.05014    1.000
    mode 2 (super, 8 frames)             0.02141    **0.427**     (1/sqrt(8) = 0.354)
    mode 3 (slow shutter, 8 integrated)  0.02144    0.428

**so the accumulation reduces the noise by 2.3x, close to the 2.83x that eight frames predict**, and the earlier
"regression I introduced" is not there either.  **And this is the second time the same configuration error produced a
confident wrong verdict** -- the three-mode test before it failed the same way -- so the lesson is recorded with the
figures rather than as a note.

**Then the shot noise, measured on a flat field where the picture's own structure cannot hide it.**  Two uniform patches,
dark 0.10 and bright 0.60, so every variation in them is noise by construction and the prediction is exact: a shot term's
standard deviation goes as `sqrt(lum)`, so the ratio should be **2.449**.

    neither                       dark 0.00487   bright 0.00774   ratio 1.590
    read 0.20 only                dark 0.00487   bright 0.00774   ratio 1.590
    shot 0.10 only                dark 0.00487   bright 0.00774   ratio 1.590
    shot 0.20 only                dark 0.00487   bright 0.00774   ratio 1.590
    read 0.20 + shot 0.20         dark 0.00487   bright 0.00774   ratio 1.590

**every row is bit-identical, which means neither term is reaching the output at all** -- not that the shot term is
weak.  **And that contradicts an earlier measurement in this same work**, where raising `read_noise` from 0 to 0.25 moved
the dark-to-bright ratio from 0.281 to 0.448.  **Two of my own measurements disagree, so at least one of them is measuring
something other than what it says**, and I could not resolve which before running out of room.  The candidates, none
checked: the source being a **synthetic flat field** rather than a photograph (so the camera's AE and AWB loops may be
driving the noise to a different operating point), the frames chosen (80-99 against 60-79), and whether the exposure loop
filters per-pixel noise while leaving the mean.

**What is therefore true of `shot_noise`**: the term exists, is read from `nv_shot_noise`, runs by default at 0.10,
implements the right law (`sigma = shot_noise * sqrt(lum)`, which is what a Poisson arrival gives), and **on a photograph
moved the dark-to-bright ratio by -0.001 where read noise moved it +0.166**.  **Whether that is the term being too weak or
the measurement being unable to see it is still open**, and the flat-field test that was meant to settle it produced a
result that contradicts a previous one instead.  **It is not claimed as verified.**

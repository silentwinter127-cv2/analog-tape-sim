settle the noise contradiction, and correct the statistic I had been judging it by

**The contradiction is resolved, and it was mine.**  On a photograph, raising `read_noise` from 0 to 0.25 moved the
dark-to-bright temporal ratio from 0.281 to 0.448.  On a flat field every noise setting gave bit-identical output.
**Taken as a whole it looked like an instrument blind spot.  It was not.**  The audit below turns both knobs that
differed between the two runs -- the source and the frame window -- and takes all six combinations:

    source / window            neither     read 0.15    read 0.30    responds?
    photograph / 60-79         0.02334     0.03157      0.03526      yes
    photograph / 80-99         0.02382     0.03211      0.03583      yes
    flat patches / 60-79       0.01284     0.02616      0.03015      yes
    flat patches / 80-99       0.01249     0.02580      0.02981      yes
    flat noise field / 60-79   0.00637     0.03727      0.04450      yes
    flat noise field / 80-99   0.00638     0.03765      0.04497      yes

**every cell responds**, so neither the source nor the window was the problem and the earlier flat-field figure was
simply measuring nothing.  **The cause was a swallowed configuration key in that figure's own parameter dictionary** --
`nv_read_noise` was dropped while the call still passed the dict, so both the "neither" and the "read 0.20" rows ran the
same configuration.  **That is the third time in this work a test of mine has been wrong about what it was running**,
and it produced a confident verdict about the model each time.

**And the shot term responds too**, on the same audit:

    photograph      shot 0.00  0.02334   shot 0.15  0.02607   shot 0.30  0.03077
    flat patches    shot 0.00  0.01284   shot 0.15  0.01978   shot 0.30  0.02706

**so it runs, in the right direction, and weaker than read noise** -- which the figure makes visible:

                        dark      bright    bright/dark
    neither             0.0058    0.0253    4.38
    read only           0.0176    0.0414    2.35
    shot only           0.0083    0.0368    4.42
    both                0.0180    0.0448    2.49

**Read noise raises the DARK region by 3.0x and the bright by 1.6x; shot noise raises the dark by only 1.4x and the
bright by 1.5x** -- i.e. **read noise dominates the shadows and shot noise the highlights, which is exactly what
`note.txt` asks for**: "加入随曝光和信号强度变化的噪声、暗部读出噪声 … 不要简单地对整张图叠加均匀颗粒；暗部和高光的表现
应该不同".  The dark x3 crops show it directly: the read-only panel is grainy in the black and the shot-only panel is
nearly clean there.

**A correction to the statistic I had been using.**  I was judging the terms by the **dark-to-bright RATIO**, and
concluded the shot term was "two orders of magnitude too weak" because it moved that ratio by 0.001.  **The ratio is the
wrong quantity**: read noise *lowers* it (adding an equal absolute amount raises the dark end proportionally more), so a
term that lowers it least looks weakest while actually being the one that dominates the highlights.  **What the note asks
for is that the two ends behave differently, which is a statement about each end and not about their quotient** -- so the
per-region figures above are the measurement, and the ratio was a statistic I had invented that hides the effect it was
meant to show.

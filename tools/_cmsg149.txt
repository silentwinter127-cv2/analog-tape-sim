confirm the striping from the pictures, and record four statistics that failed to detect it

**The user's report is confirmed, and by eye rather than by number.**  Written at 533x400 with no crop and no
enlargement, the footage on one row and the model on the next: **the real frames carry the case, the monitor and the
equipment with visible structure, and every render is covered in coarse diagonal and horizontal bands with the subject
barely legible.**  **And the bands are present in mode 0 as well**, so they come from the chain rather than from the
night-vision terms -- which is why every earlier round's calibration, all of it aimed at the modes, did not touch them.

**Four statistics were built to measure this and all four failed**, each for its own reason, and they are recorded
because reusing them would waste the next round as well:

  * **the spread of the row means** -- the first thing tried, and **blind to the thing being looked for**: a banded
    picture's row profile, low-passed, is *smoother* than a profile of ordinary content, so **the render scored 0.240
    against the footage's 0.431**, i.e. the metric said the banding was *less* than the footage while the crop showed
    more.  Ten parameter ablations were run against it and six changed nothing at all, bit for bit;
  * **the one-pixel high-pass**, meant to measure lost texture: **it says the render carries MORE detail than the
    footage (0.085 against 0.079)**, and switching the comb off collapses it to 0.003 -- so the comb is what the detail
    is coming *through*, not what is removing it;
  * **the peak-to-floor ratio of the row profile's spectrum**, meant to isolate periodicity: **both the render and the
    footage peak in bin 1 and the footage's ratio is the larger (119655 against 14924)**, so it measured the frame's
    overall vertical gradient rather than any band structure;
  * **and the parameter ablations** -- `head_switching`, `head_wear`, `comb_depth`, `chroma_vert_blend`,
    `separation_kind`, `dropout` -- because **none of them is in the banding's path**, which the bit-identical results
    said plainly and which took a round to accept.

**So the measurements cannot be used to choose between explanations and the picture is the evidence.**  That is a
pattern worth naming: **five times in this work a statistic has disagreed with the picture and the picture has been
right** -- the row-mean spread, the high-pass, the spectral ratio, the "inert" verdicts produced by swallowed
configuration keys, and the cross-configuration readings.  **Each time the fault was in what the instrument measured,
not in what the picture showed.**

**What is established about the banding itself:**

  * it is **present in every render at every mode**, so it is the chain rather than the night-vision terms;
  * it is **coarse** -- the bands are tens of pixels wide, not line-rate -- which points at something operating on the
    row structure at a large scale rather than at a per-line artefact;
  * the source photograph **does not** show it (the first panel of the side-by-side is clean), so it is added by the
    chain rather than inherited;
  * and **the stripes are diagonal**, not axis-aligned, which means whatever produces them interacts with the picture's
    content rather than being a fixed pattern.

**What is NOT established, and is not claimed**: which term produces it.  The next step is a stage trace of the *picture*

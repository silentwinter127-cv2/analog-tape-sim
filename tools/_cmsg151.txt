**Refuted: the label grid.**  The bands' spacing looked like the vision model's 8x8 grid, whose nearest-neighbour
upsampling gives 66x50-pixel cells.  **Measured, it is not**:

    grid 2  (cells 266x200)   0.15559

    grid 24 (cells 22x17)     0.14267
    **material labelling OFF  0.13540**  -- HIGHER than with it on

**so the cell size does not control the structure, and turning the labelling off makes it worse** -- the grid is not the
cause and was the seventh hypothesis to fail.

**Narrowed to three lines, by reading `apply` rather than sweeping parameters.**  The call order in `camera.apply` is:

    1043  rgb = self._night_vision(rgb, "scene", buf.index)    <- the only thing the modes differ by
    1044  rgb = self._roll_off(rgb)
    1051  rgb = self._shadow_depth(rgb)
    ...   the lens, flare and sharpen terms run in both modes

**and `_roll_off` is the first tone curve to run after the night-vision terms.**  **The night-vision terms raise the
luma from 0.1973 to 0.2303**, and a non-linear curve applied at a **different input level** redistributes structure
differently -- **so the night-vision terms need not add banding themselves; they need only move the picture into a
different part of a curve that does.**  That is consistent with every measurement made: **each night-vision piece applied
alone to the source moved the row-difference by at most 0.002**, because applied alone it does not go through
`_roll_off` at all.

**And `_roll_off` has its own history here**, recorded earlier in this work: it was measured to be an S-curve that
**raises** contrast rather than the compressor it was believed to be, and the 0.785 reading that once suggested otherwise
came from a different `dynamic_range` configuration.  **A curve that raises contrast at one input level can band at
another**, which is exactly the kind of level-dependent behaviour the mode change would expose.

fix the banding: the near-IR albedo map was upsampled with nearest-neighbour, stamping its cell edges onto the picture

**The banding was the vision model's grid, and the picture showed it in one step after nine statistics had failed.**

Tracing inside the `camera`, one image written per call:

    call               mode0      mode1      difference
    (the input)        0.12737
    **_night_vision**  0.12737   **0.19899**  **+0.07162**   <- the whole excess, in one call
    _roll_off          0.12695    0.19785    +0.07090       (and every later call leaves it unchanged)
    _shadow_depth .. _sharpen   unchanged

**so the night-vision terms make it, and `_roll_off` -- this round's first guess -- does not.**  Then the two maps the
vision model supplies were written out directly, and the answer was in the first one:

    the source                     clean, row-difference 0.0939
    **the albedo map itself**      **nothing but large diagonal bands**, row-difference 0.0670
    the illuminator field          the radial beam
    **the source multiplied by the albedo map** -- which is exactly what `ir_highlights` does -- **is the banded frame**

**The mechanism.**  `albedo_map` carried the model's **8x8 labels to the frame with `np.repeat`**, so each cell became a
**flat 66x50-pixel block** and the boundary between two cells was a **step**.  `IR_ALBEDO`'s own range is **0.28 to 2.10
-- a 7.5x step** at that boundary, so multiplying the photograph by the map **stamped the grid's edges onto the picture as
straight bands**.  **They look diagonal because the subject is**: a rotated case and monitor under an axis-aligned grid,
which is why they never looked like a grid artefact and why seven parameter ablations and two spectral statistics missed
them.

**And the comment that justified it was wrong in a way worth recording.**  It read: "bilinear would invent smooth
gradients between materials that do not exist in the table, and the model's own boundaries are the cell edges; a hard edge
is the honest rendering of a grid of class labels."  **That is honest about the GRID and wrong about the PICTURE.**  The
assumption bilinear adds is that a material boundary lies somewhere between two cell centres and its position is unknown --
**which is exactly true at an 8x8 labelling of a 533x400 frame**, and a far smaller error than a 7.5x step at a guessed
line.  **The labels are unchanged**: this is the map's rendering, not the model's classification.

**Measured after the fix:**

    the real footage                  0.07562
    mode 1, before                    0.13124
    **mode 1, after**                 **0.11036**
    mode 1, after, shot_noise off     0.10523
    mode 0 for reference              0.10040

**so mode 1 moves from 27% above the reference to 46% of the way back**, and the picture confirms it: the coarse diagonal
bands are gone and the subject is legible, where before it was not.

**Why nine statistics failed on this and the picture did not.**  The row-mean spread, the one-pixel high-pass, the
peak-to-floor spectral ratio, row-difference, and five configuration errors -- **every one of them disagreed with what the
eye could see**, and the eye was right each time.  **The instrument that worked was writing the intermediate images and
looking at them**, which located it to a single call on the first attempt and to a single map on the second.  That is now
the recorded method for a picture-level defect.

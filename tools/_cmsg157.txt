**so the level is not the cause -- it accounts for 0.0006 where the camera's addition is 0.038 -- and the shot-noise term
alone accounts for far more than the whole of it.**  In the chain the `player` reduces that contribution to 0.005, which is
why switching it off moves the render by so little while it dominates the camera's own figure.

**And that is what the shot-noise term is for**, so this is not a defect in it: `note.txt` asks for noise that varies with
the signal, and a per-pixel field scaled by `sqrt(lum)` does exactly that.  **The finding is about the MEASURE**: `rowsig`
responds to per-pixel noise and hardly at all to level, so **the camera's "+0.038" and the render's "0.10953 against the
footage's 0.07562" were never measurements of banding** -- they were measurements of noise against a normalising spread.

**A tenth metric, and it failed too.**  A proper banding measure was built -- the strongest autocorrelation peak of the
row-mean profile beyond lag 3, which is periodic structure and immune to per-pixel noise because the row means average it
away -- and **it peaked at lag 3 for every image including the footage and the input** (0.9341-0.9768 across all of them,
with no separation whatsoever).  **So it measured the low-frequency content of the row profile, not periodicity**, and it
is recorded as failed rather than used.

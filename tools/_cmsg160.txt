**The diagnosis, traced call by call.**  The model's brightest pixel is **0.8686 with nothing clipped** where the footage
reaches **1.0 with 0.66% blown out**, and the mean is right -- so the loss is confined to the top of the curve.  A flat
field traced through the camera:

    call                input 0.90   input 1.00   difference
    (the source)        0.9000       1.0000       +0.1000
    _expose             0.9000       1.0000       +0.1000
    _night_vision scene 0.9999       1.0000       +0.0001
    _roll_off           1.0000       1.0000       +0.0000     <- the shoulder is fine
    ...
    **_night_vision disp 0.8532       0.8532       +0.0000**   <- everything is lost here

**and `_roll_off` is exonerated** -- its analytic curve maps 0.90 to 0.944 and 1.00 to 1.000, exactly as it should.  The
scene branch reaches 0.9999 and the shoulder finishes the job; **the display branch flattens both to 0.8532.**

**The mechanism is arithmetic, not physical.**  `monochrome_green`'s weights sum to 1, so their largest is **0.4294** -- a
full-scale luminance cannot push any channel past 0.43 -- and because the sum is 1, reaching an output luminance of 1.0
would require **all three channels at 1.0**, i.e. pure white.  **So a display built this way can never reach the top of its
own range**, which is why the render's maximum is 0.8686 and no pixel anywhere clips.

**`note.txt` asks for exactly this** -- "加入 … 以及高亮区域的饱和" -- **and it was the one of its six mechanisms with no
implementation.**

**What was implemented, and why it is only PARTLY closed.**  `highlight_saturation` drives the projection so the peak
channel can pass full scale and clip there, and it does move the top:

    drive   mean     p90      p99      max      clipped
    1.00    0.2370   0.5499   0.8061   0.8686   0.00000
    1.35    0.3060   0.7163   0.8844   0.9366   0.00000
    1.90    0.3964   0.8474   0.9481   0.9752   0.00022
    the footage  0.2346   0.5870   0.9511   **1.0000**  **0.00657**

**but it is a global gain**, so it raises every level with the top.  Twenty drive-and-exposure pairs were scored on all
three targets together and **none reaches them**: the best is drive 1.00 with exposure 0.00, which is the model unchanged.
**A global gain cannot make a small part of the frame clip while the rest does not, and that is what saturation means** --
so the knob's shape is wrong and **its default is 1.0, i.e. it does not act**.  The mechanism is recorded as
**partly implemented and not calibrated**, not as fixed.

**A first attempt that failed, recorded**: normalising the weights by their **maximum** instead of their sum.  It put the
peak channel at full scale, and **broke the luminance** -- measured, the mean fell from 0.2373 to 0.1907, because
`weights @ LUMA == 1` is exactly the constraint that makes `mono` carry the picture's brightness and the whole R:G:B
calibration rests on it.

**`illuminator` was fixed in the same round**, and it is the sixth parameter in this work found to have no effect: 0.15
through 3.00 gave bit-identical output, and the field itself was identical at both ends (min 0.05475, max 0.06625).  **And
unlike the other five the value DID arrive** -- a probe shows 0.15 reaching `add_illuminator` as 0.150 -- so the fault was
the arithmetic: the field was normalised by its mean and multiplied by a **fixed 0.062**, never by `illuminator`.  It now
scales, with the default at 1.20 reproducing the previous picture (`0.062 * 1.20` against the old constant), and the mean
lands at **0.2370 against the footage's 0.2346**.

**`tools/calib/FINAL_MODES.png`** is the deliverable: the input, the three real frames and the three modes with each
panel's own mean, percentiles, maximum, clipped fraction, hue spread and saturation printed beside it.

find why a new parameter is dropped, implement the saturation point, and record that it is still only partly closed

**The mechanism behind six inert parameters, found.**  `saturation_stops` was added to the camera and produced
**bit-identical output at 0.0, 0.3, 0.6 and 0.9** -- the sixth time in this work a parameter has had no effect.  A probe
inside `_roll_off` showed the attribute was **always 0.00** however it was configured, and the cause is one step earlier:

    merge_values(BASE, {"camera": {"saturation_stops": 0.6}})
        -> camera.saturation_stops = 0.6
    SCHEMA.resolve_all(that)
        -> camera.saturation_stops = **ABSENT**

    for contrast, three declared parameters survive both: dynamic_range, nv_chroma_residue, nv_illuminator

**`SCHEMA.resolve_all` rebuilds the layers from the schema's declarations and DROPS every key it does not know**, and
`Project.resolved_params` uses it -- so **a new parameter must be declared in the schema or it never reaches the stage.**
**That is the common step behind the six**: a value exists in the config and something between the config and the term
removes it.  It is recorded in the schema help beside the parameter, because the next new parameter will meet it too.

**And the diagnosis of the highlights holds.**  `_roll_off` clips correctly at its white point and is not the cause; the
cause is that **nothing reaches the white point**, because at the default `dynamic_range` it is exactly 1.0 while the
picture's own maximum is 0.87.  So the saturation point is now exposed directly, in stops below the range's own white --
**a sensor's saturation point is a property of the sensor, not of its dynamic range**, and `dynamic_range` moves it the
wrong way (a lower range raises the white point).

**Measured, with the parameter finally reaching the term:**

    sat_stops   white    clipping on a flat field
    0.00        1.0000   0.01734
    0.30        0.8123   0.06842
    0.60        0.6598   0.13746
    0.90        0.5359   0.21363

**so it works and it clips where it should.**  On the real photograph, though, its reach is small:

    sat_stops   mean     p99      max      clipped
    0.00        0.2370   0.8061   0.8686   0.00000
    0.30        0.2429   0.8334   0.8857   0.00000
    0.40        0.2449   0.8412   0.8882   0.00000
    the footage  0.2346   0.9511   **1.0000**  **0.00657**

**so it raises the maximum by 0.017 and still clips nothing.**  The curve redistributes energy into the shoulder rather
than pinning it, because the photograph's brightest pixel is only at 0.87 -- **to blow out, the picture must have a
brighter highlight to begin with**, which is a property of the light rather than of the curve.

**And the default is 0.0, because 0.30 broke a check.**  The suite caught that the scanline structure began costing light
once the white point was lowered -- **a verified property traded for 0.017 of maximum is not a trade worth making**, so
the parameter is left reachable and inactive, and the shortfall is recorded rather than papered over.  **That is what the
163 checks are for**, and this round is the first in some time where one of them stopped a change rather than confirming
one.

**163 checks pass, selftest clean.**

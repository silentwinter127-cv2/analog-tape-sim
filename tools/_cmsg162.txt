**And the reason is the input, not the chain.**  The lamp **scales the whole field uniformly**, so it multiplies the
picture's highlight-to-midtone ratio by one -- and that ratio is what decides whether anything saturates.  The photograph's
brightest pixel sits at 0.87 of full scale while the footage's saturates; **no uniform gain can change a ratio.**  The
footage has 0.66% of its frame blown out because the camcorder's own lamp was illuminating surfaces that returned more
than full scale during the exposure -- **a property of that scene under that light, which a visible-light photograph of a
different moment does not contain.**

**This is the honest end of the highlight line of enquiry**, and it is worth stating what it does and does not mean:

  * **it does not mean the model has no saturation mechanism.**  `_roll_off` clips correctly and `saturation_stops` moves
    the clip point -- measured on flat fields, a 0.90 input goes from 0.01734 clipped at 0 to **0.21363** at 0.90 stops.
    Both work;
  * **it means this input cannot reach them.**  A source whose brightest pixel is 0.87 and whose top decile spans 0.15 of
    the range will not saturate however it is gained;
  * **and it means the earlier verdicts stand**: the model's whole-frame figures match the footage (mean 0.2399 against
    0.2346, hue spread and saturation inside the footage's ranges), and the highlight shortfall is **inherited from what
    the model was given** rather than added by it -- the same conclusion the row-structure test reached from the other
    direction, where the chain was found to be *reducing* what it was handed.

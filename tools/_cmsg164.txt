**The user's report is confirmed and now explained numerically, and the explanation is arithmetic rather than a missing
term.**  With the moment projection removed from `ir_highlights` so that its over-range survives to the ceiling -- measured,
the function's raw output has **1.2% of its pixels above full scale** -- the chain's maximum is **still 0.8711** where the
footage reaches **1.0000**.

**And the reason is the display's own weights.**  `monochrome_green` builds the picture as `lum * [0.2168, 0.4294, 0.3538]`
with the ratio identical for every pixel, so with the **green channel pinned at full scale** the luminance is

    0.2168 * 0.505  +  0.587 * 1.000  +  0.114 * 0.824  =  0.958

**and reaching a luminance of 1.0 requires all three channels at full scale -- pure white.**  **A projection that scales
the three channels together can never produce white**, by construction, and that is a ceiling the model has been sitting
under the whole time.  The measured maximum of 0.8686 is below even that 0.958 ceiling, which the over-range removal in
`ir_highlights` accounts for.

**The mechanism that would lift it is two-regime and is what a display actually does**: as the drive rises the strongest
channel saturates first and the others keep climbing, so **a bright highlight goes white and desaturates towards the top**
while the mid-tones keep the measured green.  **That is exactly what the footage shows** -- a green cast everywhere and
blown highlights -- and it is `note.txt`'s "高亮区域的饱和", the one of its six mechanisms with no implementation.

**An attempt at it was made and it did not fire, and it is reverted rather than left in.**  The gate was written as
`white_at = wmax / drive`, which puts the transition where the *brightest weight* reaches full scale -- and since `wmax` is
0.4294 and the picture's own maximum luminance is 0.87, the gate never opened.  **The correct threshold follows from the
arithmetic above: the push must begin where the GREEN channel reaches full scale, i.e. at `lum = 1 / 0.587 = 1.70`**, which
this photograph's brightest pixel never reaches either -- so even a correctly placed gate would not fire on this input, and
the honest conclusion is the same one the lamp sweep reached from the other direction: **to show a blown highlight the
picture must contain a highlight bright enough to blow, and this source's brightest pixel is at 0.87.**

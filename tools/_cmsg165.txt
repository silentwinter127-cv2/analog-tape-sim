fix the highlights: the drive now acts only ABOVE the gate, and both the mean and the top move the right way

**The user's report -- "现在的高光仍然不够明显" -- was right, and the cause was arithmetic.**  With the moment projection
removed from `ir_highlights` so its over-range survives, the chain's maximum was **still 0.8711** where the footage reaches
**1.0000**.  And the reason was the display's own ratio: `lum * [0.2168, 0.4294, 0.3538]` is applied identically to every
pixel, so even with green pinned at full scale the luminance is **0.958** -- and reaching 1.0 needs **all three channels at
full scale**, i.e. white.  **A projection that scales the three channels together can never produce white.**

**What a display actually does is two-regime, and the gate is derivable.**  A channel saturates where `lum * w == 1`; with
the weights normalised so `weights @ LUMA == 1` they are **[0.6069, 1.2021, 0.9904]**, so:

    green pins at   lum = 1 / 1.2021 = **0.832**
    blue  pins at   lum = 1 / 0.9904 = 1.010
    red   pins at   lum = 1 / 0.6069 = 1.648

**and the brightest luminance the scene branch delivers is 0.8225** -- within 1% of the green gate.  So above the gate the
strongest channel is pinned while the others keep climbing with the drive, the pixel **desaturates towards white**, and its
luminance leaves the 0.958 ceiling.  **That is `note.txt`'s "高亮区域的饱和", the one of its six mechanisms that had no
implementation.**

**And the gate is what makes it work.**  The first version multiplied the drive into every pixel, which is what the earlier
`nv_highlight_saturation` did -- **and a gain applied where nothing is saturating does not make a highlight, it makes a
brighter photograph**: measured, gain 1.0 to 1.6 took the mean from 0.2370 to **0.3493**.  With the drive acting **only
above the gate**:

    gain   mean     p90      p99      max      clipped   score
    1.00   0.2370   0.5499   0.8061   0.8686   0.00000   0.3135     <- as it was
    **2.00   0.2383   0.5508   0.8454   0.9799   0.00000   0.1663**
    the footage  0.2346   0.5870   0.9511   **1.0000**  0.00657

**so the mean moves by +0.5% while the maximum rises by 0.111 and the 99th percentile by 0.039**, and the score -- which
weights the mean, the percentiles, the maximum and the clipped fraction together -- **halves**.  Above gain 2.0 nothing
further changes, because the pixels above the gate are already fully clipped.

**This is the first time in this work that the top of the range has been raised without brightening the picture**, and it
is the mechanism the user asked for.  What is still short is stated plainly: the maximum reaches **0.9799 against 1.0000**
and **nothing clips** where the footage has **0.66%** blown out -- **so the highlights are visible now and still not
saturated**, which is a consequence of the input (brightest pixel at 0.87 of full scale) that the lamp and saturation-point
sweeps each established from a different direction.

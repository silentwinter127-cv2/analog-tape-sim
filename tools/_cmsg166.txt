**The new photograph was the input the previous conclusion called for, and it works.**  The earlier photograph's brightest
pixel was at 0.87 of full scale with no emitter in frame -- reflected light only -- so nothing could saturate however the
curve was shaped.  **This one has lit LED fans in the subject, so it contains a light source**, and with the gated drive
already in place:

    the footage                       mean 0.2346   p90 0.5870   p99 0.9511   max 1.0000   clipped **0.00657**
    the input itself                  mean 0.2118   p90 0.5457   p99 0.7738   max 1.0000   clipped 0.00007
    **the model, as it ships**        mean 0.2543   p90 0.5794   p99 0.7963   max 0.9104   clipped **0.00000**
    sat 0.8 / exposure -0.15          mean 0.2573   p90 0.6274   p99 0.9202   max **0.9973**   clipped **0.00226**
    **sat 1.0 / exposure -0.30**      mean **0.2373**  p90 0.5681   p99 0.8705   max **0.9920**   clipped **0.00064**

**so the engine can now produce a blown highlight -- 0.00064 to 0.00226 of the frame where before it produced none at all**
-- and `sat 1.0 / exposure -0.30` puts the mean within **1.2%** of the footage's and the 90th percentile within **3.2%**.

**And that setting is NOT the default, because making it the default broke four checks.**  Lowering the sensor's saturation
point by a stop changed the tone curve everywhere, and the suite caught the consequences:

    [FAIL] auto-exposure reaches the rendered picture        largest luma difference against no loop: 0.0146
    [FAIL] the scanline structure costs no light             the frame's mean varies by 0.00275 across the four spot sizes
    [FAIL] a blown highlight has a dark ring, scaling with the overflow drain   drain 0.15 -> 0.00000 darker at radius 0
    [FAIL] the drain darkens a ring and not the picture      the frame's mean changes by +0.00000 while the ring deepens

**Those are properties verified earlier in this work -- the exposure loop's own behaviour, the scanline structure not
costing light, and the overflow drain's dark ring -- and trading them for a highlight is exactly what the 163 checks
exist to prevent.**  So the mechanism ships **reachable and not imposed**: the default is `saturation_stops = 0.0`, and the
calibration that reaches the footage's highlights is recorded in the parameter's help with these figures.

**That is the honest state of the highlights**, and it is a real change from where this started:

  * **the mechanism exists and is derived from the display's own arithmetic** -- a channel saturates where `lum * w == 1`,
    which for the normalised weights puts green at **0.832**, and the drive acts only above that gate so the mid-tone
    calibration fitted to the footage is untouched;
  * **it can blow out** -- measured, clipped 0.00064 to 0.00226 against the footage's 0.00657, where the model previously
    clipped **nothing** at any setting;
  * **and the remaining gap is a trade against four verified properties**, which is a decision for whoever tunes the
    instrument rather than something to be imposed by default.

**163 checks pass, selftest clean.**

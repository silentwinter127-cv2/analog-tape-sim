raise the drive to its true plateau, and record what "still not bright enough" actually measures

**The drive default was set below its plateau.**  Measured on the new photograph, the gated drive saturates at 4.0, not
2.0 -- 6.0 and 8.0 give bit-identical results -- so the default was leaving the top of the range short for no reason:

    gain   mean     p90      p99      max      clipped   score
    2.0    0.2543   0.5794   0.7963   0.9104   0.00000   0.3687     <- the old default
    4.0    0.2554   0.5811   0.8238   0.9648   0.00000   0.2874     <- the plateau
    the footage  0.2346   0.5870   0.9511   1.0000   0.00657

**so the default is now 4.0**, which costs nothing -- 163 checks pass unchanged -- and moves the maximum from 0.9104 to
**0.9648** and the 99th percentile from 0.7963 to **0.8238**.

**And the report is worth measuring rather than answering with "brighter", because the mean is not the deficit.**  The
model's mean is **0.2554 against the footage's 0.2346** -- it is already **9% brighter on average** -- while its **p90 is
0.5811 against 0.5870 and its p99 is 0.8238 against 0.9511**.  **So what reads as "not bright enough" is the top of the
range being compressed, not the picture being dim**: the average is right and the bright parts are held back, which is
exactly the defect the whole highlight line of enquiry has been about.

**And the setting that closes the rest of it is known, measured, and deliberately not the default:**

    sat 1.0 / exposure -0.30    mean 0.2373   p90 0.5681   p99 0.8705   max 0.9920   clipped 0.00064
    sat 0.8 / exposure -0.15    mean 0.2573   p90 0.6274   p99 0.9202   max 0.9973   clipped 0.00226
    the footage                 mean 0.2346   p90 0.5870   p99 0.9511   max 1.0000   clipped 0.00657

**-- it breaks four checks**: the exposure loop's own behaviour, the scanline structure costing no light, and the overflow
drain's dark ring.  **Those are properties verified earlier in this work**, and the suite refuses the trade, which is what
it is for.

**So the honest summary of the highlights at this point:**

  * **the mechanism is implemented and derived from the display's arithmetic** -- a channel saturates where `lum * w == 1`,
    green at **0.832** for the normalised weights, and the drive acts only above that gate so the mid-tone calibration is
    untouched;
  * **it is at its plateau by default** and the maximum reaches **0.9648** against the footage's 1.0000;
  * **the remaining gap needs `saturation_stops`, and that is a trade against four verified properties** -- a decision for
    whoever tunes the instrument, with the numbers recorded in the parameter's help;
  * **and part of the gap is the input**: this photograph's own p99 is **0.7738** against the footage's **0.9511**, so even
    its brightest regions are less extreme than the footage's, and no curve can put into the picture contrast that the
    photograph does not contain.

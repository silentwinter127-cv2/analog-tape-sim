**The comparison that is actually fair was run, and it found a real systematic error -- but not the one I predicted.**
The calibration had been fitted against a photograph of an **RGB case with red, magenta and pink LED emitters**, and those
are *visible-light* sources: under this camcorder's NightShot -- IR-cut filter removed, near-infrared lamp on -- they
contribute to the picture in a way that a visible-light photograph does not record.  **So no tuning on that input could
reach the footage**, and the render's `R/G` of 1.34 against the footage's 0.37-0.85 is what that looks like.

**And the footage's own spread is now measured**, one frame per second across the whole stretch:

    t = 52-53s    mean 0.11-0.18   R/G 0.85-0.81     (a transitional moment)
    t = 54-67s    mean 0.22-0.30   R/G 0.49-0.64     (NIGHTSHOT 1)
    t = 68-82s    mean 0.33-0.39   R/G 0.37-0.55     (NIGHTSHOT 2)

**so the target is a range and a single reference frame was the wrong thing to fit** -- which is why "看上去不一样" kept
returning with a different frame each time.  **The user chose to calibrate against the range**, and that is recorded here.

**The fair test feeds each real NightShot frame into the chain and compares the output with that same frame.**  Under it,
my hypothesis -- that `ir_highlights` was double-applying the near-infrared response to content that had already received
it -- **is refuted**:

    t(s)    the real frame            ir_response 1.0        ir_response 0.0
           mean    R/G               mean    R/G            mean    R/G
    52     0.1087  0.849             0.1259  0.729          0.1378  0.744
    60     0.2422  0.587             0.2653  0.687          0.2819  0.711
    72     0.3597  0.449             0.3936  0.695          0.4031  0.704
    78     0.3867  0.367             0.4229  0.645          0.4333  0.665

**turning the near-infrared response OFF is worse at every single point** -- both the mean and the ratio move further from
the input.  **So `nv_ir_response = 1.0` stays the default**, and the schema help now carries this measurement instead of
the argument I had written into it a few minutes earlier.  **A refuted hypothesis must not remain in the instrument's own
documentation as if it were a reason.**

**What the same table exposes instead, and it is systematic**: the mean comes out **about 12% high at every timestamp**
while the input frame's own mean is already correct, and `R/G` is **consistently higher** than the input's.  **The input is
already a night-vision output -- its mean and its ratios were right when it arrived -- and the chain lays another tone
curve over it.**  That is the defect, it is not in `ir_highlights`, and **it is not located yet**; it is recorded with the
figures rather than guessed at.

**`tools/real_input.py` reproduces the whole table**, so the next attempt starts from the measurement instead of the
argument.

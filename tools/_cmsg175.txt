audit note.txt against the implementation: 18 requirements, 11 present, 5 partial, 2 absent

**`note.txt` was re-read and every one of its 18 claims was answered by running the engine**, because this work's own record
says the dangerous failure is a mechanism that *looks* implemented.  `tools/note_audit.py` reproduces the whole table.

**The file is two halves** -- a chroma-damage model (its sections 1-9) and a NightShot physical model (its second half plus
a recommended node graph) -- so the two are audited apart.

**ABSENT (2), and neither is a tuning fault:**

  * **temporal chroma lag** (`C(t-1)` blended into the current chroma).  There is no `nv_chroma_lag` in the schema and the
    spec's 23 fields contain no previous-frame chroma, so the term does not exist.
  * **infrared focus shift** -- blur applied to the IR layer alone.  **And my own attempt at it was measured and
    reverted.**  The note calls this out as something most people do not know, and it is an *optical* mechanism, which is
    this model's strength, so I implemented it: a Gaussian blur of the IR response followed by the term's own mean
    restoration, **claiming the mean was untouched by construction.  Measured, the mean went from 0.3750 to 0.9697 at
    shift 2.0** -- blurring concentrates energy at low frequencies and the spread-normalisation and ratio that follow
    amplify it, so the level contract was broken.  **A term that breaks the level is worse than an absent one**, so it is
    reverted rather than shipped broken, and the failure is recorded with its numbers.

**PARTIAL (5), each with what is missing named:** the chroma resolution limit is applied by the player at 0.75 MHz but the
night-vision path adds the residue at full resolution; the residue is now hue-masked per pixel but has no radial or edge
term, where the note asks for the anomaly to grow towards the frame's edge; `Chroma degradation` sits in `player`, i.e. the
**decoder**, rather than before the codec; `optics.blooming` exists with its threshold and amount but sits in the
**objective** acting on the whole picture rather than at the sensor spilling from a saturated photosite; and the green
matrix exists **but the dark end is scaled rather than lifted** (0.02 in becomes 0.0159 / 0.0220 / 0.0202) where the note
asks for black to be a deep green grey.

**PRESENT (11):** YUV chroma processing; **per-channel blooming** (measured: a red-only patch spills R, G and B to the
corners independently, which is what turns the edge of a red highlight cyan); phase error, on this work's list of
unreached parameters; damage before compression; the parameterised node (24 `nv_*` parameters); green from a display
matrix rather than a filter; IR as a per-material spectral response; the lamp's falloff, **which this work found multiplied
by a fixed constant and never by the parameter**; photon noise as `sqrt` plus separate read noise; slow-shutter
integration taken from the END of the window so the trail is behind the subject; and the OSD inside the chain.

**And the note's own last line is the thing it cares most about** -- "最大的特点其实不是绿色，而是红外世界重新定义了材质明暗
关系" -- **and that one is implemented**: `ir_highlights` multiplies the linear luminance by the per-material albedo, so
what the picture shows is the *material ordering*, and the map spans **5.8x (0.344 to 2.001)** where foliage at 2.10
against dark clothing at 0.28 is precisely the 7.5x ratio the luminance curve cannot express.

**Two corrections to my own audit, both found by running it**: it first reported per-channel blooming ABSENT because it
looked for `blooming` in the night-vision codec when it lives in `atsim/codecs/optics.py` and the camera calls it -- **the
instrument being wrong rather than the engine**, which is this work's twelfth instance of that fault class.

**`REVIEW.md` gains a 5.5 section** carrying the table and four questions for the reviewer, **including whether my reading
of the two PARTIAL position faults is a real distinction or pedantry**.

**163 checks pass, selftest clean, working tree clean.**

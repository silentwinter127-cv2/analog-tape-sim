add receiver Y/C separation topologies: box, 1H, 2H and 2-D FIR

ntsc-rs makes its chroma demodulator a *choice of filter topology*, and that choice is what produces
the coloured fringes on high-contrast edges in the reference panel:

    box      luma = mean of four samples spanning +-1 px
    1-line   luma = this line and the previous
    2-line   luma = 0.5*this + 0.25*prev + 0.25*next
    2-D FIR  +-1 px horizontally and +-1 line vertically

This model had one topology -- a 1H delay-line comb mixed with a band split -- so it could not produce
the box or 2-D FIR fringe shapes at all. These are real receiver designs of different eras rather than
effect presets: a cheap set uses a band split, a better one a glass delay line, a good one two lines
or a 2-D comb.

**Why this is a mechanism and not a filter choice bolted on.** A comb works because the subcarrier
inverts on alternate lines, so a tap taken one line away arrives antiphase and cancels the carrier.
When the taps are taken at the right phase the cancellation is *automatic* -- no depth coefficient and
no normalisation factor. The taps are chosen that way, and chroma is simply `composite - luma`, so any
error in the luma estimate lands in the colour as cross-colour. That is exactly what a real separator
produces, and it is where the edge fringes come from.

Measured separation quality on a synthetic composite (chroma energy; lower is better separation):

    band  0.1857     box  0.1164     fir2d  0.0686     line1  0.0493     line2  0.0264

and through the whole renderer every topology produces a distinct picture, with `kind = 0` matching
the previous behaviour pixel for pixel, so nothing existing changes.

**Three interface faults on the way in, all caught by running the code:**

  * the parameter was added to the `format` layer while the player reads its own layer, so it fell
    back to its default and was unchangeable -- the "reachable but inert" pattern again;
  * the read was inserted above the line binding `d`, raising NameError on construction; the player's
    own layer is `p` and `f` is the format layer. This is the second time in this session that a
    parameter read failed because of *where* it was placed rather than what it said -- the CRT
    moisture block was inserted above `super().__init__()` and broke construction the same way;
  * the schema stores it numerically for the slider while the function takes a name, and the seam
    between the two was missing. The mapping now lives beside the topology tuple so the numbering and
    the order cannot drift.

**Two wrong turns in the 2-D FIR itself**, both worth recording because both were arithmetic that
looked plausible:

  * the taps did not sum to one. Measured, `fir2d` returned a luma mean of 0.4408 against 0.3527 for
    every other topology -- a 25% lift from a DC gain of about 1.25, because a "diagonal correction"
    term I had written was not a convolution at all. A filter whose taps do not sum to one is a
    brightness control with a misleading name, so the weights are now accumulated in the same loop
    that applies them and the total cannot disagree with the taps.
  * the vertical tap used `shifted[dy:] = plane[:dy]` with `dy = -1`, which Python reads as
    `shifted[-1:] = plane[:-1]` -- one row into one row, which raised a broadcast error and would not
    have shifted anything even if the shapes had matched. It is one `np.roll` now.

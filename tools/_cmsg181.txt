follow the YUV colour space, which is the architectural fault behind the last four "band-aid" attempts

**The user's instruction names the actual defect: `YUV色彩空间应该遵循`.**  Everything the night-vision display did was expressed
in **RGB**, where the cast is a fixed per-channel gain on a luminance -- and **that cannot express what a display does**,
because a green display is a statement about its chroma channels, not about its R:G:B gains: it passes luminance almost
unchanged and attenuates the two chroma components, **each by its own factor**.  The RGB form is a special case reachable
only when U and V share a scale, **which is why `chroma_residue` had to be invented and why it then behaved strangely**: it
was carrying *chroma* through a path built to carry *gain*.

**So the residue moved into the space the mechanism lives in**, BT.601 as the standard defines it:

    Y =  0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B        U = (B - Y) * 0.564        V = (R - Y) * 0.713

**and the direction of the two chroma gains is the whole mechanism**, which took two attempts to get right.  A green display
is a display whose blue channel is weak, and in this space the blue difference is **U** -- so the cast is **U suppressed
harder than V**, which is `note.txt`'s `U减少 / V偏移` read literally.  **The first version had them the other way round**
(U 0.70, V 1.30) and measured **`R/G 1.000, B/G 1.000` on a neutral input** -- it produced GREY, because passing more V than
U pulls the result towards red while the display pulls it towards green.  The measurements that settle it:

    the display projection alone, on a neutral grey    #658D81   R/G 0.721   B/G 0.917
    the real NIGHTSHOT 2                               #27473B   R/G 0.556   B/G 0.838
    the real NIGHTSHOT 1                               #38564C   R/G 0.649   B/G 0.884

**so the projection is already the right shape and slightly too pale, and the residue must push it further in the SAME
direction rather than replace it.**  A second correction was needed for that: the first version built the output straight
from `lumv` and the input's own chroma, which **discarded the display projection** -- measured, a neutral grey input came
out `R/G 1.000, B/G 1.000`, the display's green replaced by the input's neutrality.  The projection is now the base and the
residue a perturbation on top of it.

**And the luminance is carried by identity rather than by argument.**  Y is `lum`, and the inverse re-solves G from the
Y equation, so **no scale on U or V can move the mean** -- verified, a neutral grey reads `luma 0.50000` at residue 0.00,
0.15, 0.45 and 1.00, **exactly**.  Every earlier version of this term had to *argue* that the mean was held; this one gets
it from the algebra.

**Measured on a bare photograph, which is the input the user asked for:**

    the basement, mode 2    primary #24322D   R/G 0.715   B/G 0.914
    the real NIGHTSHOT 1    primary #38564C   R/G 0.649   B/G 0.884
    the real NIGHTSHOT 2    primary #27473B   R/G 0.556   B/G 0.838

**so the primary's structure is finally right**: `G` largest, `B` middle, `R` smallest, where the previous render read
`#4A5869` -- **blue largest**.  `R/G` 0.715 sits in the neighbourhood of the two targets and `B/G` 0.914 is close to
0.838-0.884.

**What is still not right is named rather than left to be found**: the hue spread **within the brightest band** reads
**0.206** where the footage reads **1.084** -- so the **rainbow fringe has not appeared yet**.  That is the next physical
step and the mechanism for it already exists: `lens.chromatic_aberration` displaces R and B about the optical axis and its
own docstring gives the reason it is the right one -- *"blurring the channels by different amounts gives a coloured haze; a
lens gives a coloured fringe, because the red and blue images arrive at slightly different sizes."*  **And it was invisible
in every earlier measurement of mine because my test rigs set `chroma_aberration = 0` alongside the other optics terms.**

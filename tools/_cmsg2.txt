measure field-to-field wander, and a hypothesis it refutes

Every tracker so far subtracted a local vertical average, because a uniform shift of a
field is not visible as a shift. That makes them blind to the component a time-base
corrector would most obviously be removing, so this measures the shared term directly:
each field is cross-correlated against a reference, giving one number per field for how
far the whole field sits from where it was. Gradients are used rather than intensities,
because a field's brightness varies for reasons unrelated to time base.

Measured on the JVC capture between 960s and 1200s, a stretch spanning both the stable and
the unstable regions:

    field-to-field wander    spread 0.074 px, range 0.3 px
    within-field variation   median 0.665 px, mean 2.845 px

**That refutes the hypothesis it was built to test.** The previous conclusion was that the
corrector's 8.8x reduction must come from field-to-field wander, since the within-field fast
component measured small. The wander measures *smaller* than the within-field variation --
0.074 px against a 0.665 px median -- so it cannot carry an 8.8x reduction either, and the
previous note was wrong.

What the numbers do show is a bimodal within-field distribution: most fields sit at 0.3 to
0.5 px, with occasional excursions to 4, 6, 8 and 10 px. That is the shape of an
intermittent fault rather than a continuous one, which matters for the model -- a mechanism
that scales smoothly cannot produce it.

Two limits are worth stating rather than working around:

  * the field-to-field comparison is against a reference field ten seconds away, so it
    measures *slow* wander. A corrector may act on much shorter time scales, and this
    measurement would not see that;
  * `within_field_spread` compares 32-row bands, so it is a coarse measure of the
    per-line variation a corrector acts on, not that variation itself.

So the real cause of the 8.8x is unresolved, and both of the structures the model has for
it -- a smooth field-wide term and a fast per-line term -- are now measured smaller than
needed on this material. Recording that is more useful than a third hypothesis.

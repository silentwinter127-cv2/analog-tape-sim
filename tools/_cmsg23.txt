add the operator: a hand holding the camera, and the lens controls it moves

The note's seventh dimension, and it calls it the most easily overlooked and the most real: the equipment
does not run itself. Everything else in this model is a property of a device; this is a property of the
person holding it, and it is what makes footage read as a recording of something rather than as a picture
with faults applied.

**Handheld shake is not noise, and representing it as noise is the mistake.** The obvious implementation
is a random offset per frame; human tremor is narrowband and rhythmic, and the rhythm is what makes a
shake read as a hand rather than as a glitch. A hand moves as a sum of quasi-periodic components from
different parts of the body -- postural sway at 0.9 Hz largest, walking and breathing at 2.1, arm and
shoulder at 5.5, physiological tremor at 9.5 smallest. Their amplitudes fall with frequency, sway being
twelve times the tremor, and **that ratio** is what the eye reads. Measured: spectral concentration
0.2205 against 0.00794 for a flat spectrum, 28x, and lag-1 correlation of the movement +0.939 against
about zero for white noise -- both asserted on the model's own trace, and the second also on the rendered
frames.

**Jostles are episodes, so they are events.** A step, a door, someone's elbow. Planned as a Poisson
process like the tape's dropouts and the deck's stiction, with a duration long enough to contain both the
jerk and its recovery. Measured: 26 events over 10 s, peak 6.84 px against an 80th-percentile of
0.795 px.

**The zoom motor is not linear.** A small DC motor and a gear train: slow while it overcomes stiction,
fast through the middle, and switched off abruptly at the end because friction holds position. Middle
slope 0.91, end slope 1.70 -- a switched-off motor still has speed, which is exactly what distinguishes
it from an ease-out.

**Two parameters were inert, and one of them was the whole focus feature.** `zoom_travel` was read into an
attribute and never used. And the focus loop computed a motor position that nothing read, because
`CameraStage` has no defocus path at all: it softens the picture through `_lens_wear`, which models
coating damage -- contrast loss, a veiling haze, desaturation. A loop that hunts for focus and does not
move the lens has not modelled hunting. So being out of focus gets its own path, a Gaussian whose radius
grows with defocus, because a misfocused lens convolves the image while a worn coating scatters light and
lifts the blacks: keeping them separate means a camera can be sharp with a hazy lens or hazy with a sharp
lens, which are two different faults. Measured after the fix: sharpness varies by 22% of its maximum over
a clip with the loop on.

This is the seventh "reachable but inert" fault found in this project, and it was found by setting the
parameter and measuring the output rather than by reading the code.

**A test that could not fail.** The first jostle test asked for 0.4 events per second over 40 frames --
an expected count of 0.64, so a zero was the most likely outcome, and the test measured nothing while
appearing to exercise the feature. The rate and length now put the expected count well above one.

Six checks added: the spectrum is narrowband; consecutive frames are correlated; jostles are spikes over a
quiet floor; a camera with no operator is unchanged; the hand reaches the picture; zoom travel softens it;
and the focus loop moves the lens.

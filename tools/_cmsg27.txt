add the recursive vertical chroma filter, the one mechanism the reference needed

Measured the NTSC-RS reference clip before building anything, and the measurements changed what to build
twice.

**What the reference actually is**, from five extracted frames of 768x576 at 30 fps -- a 525-line signal
resampled, so its subcarrier is not at the original frequency and any phase measurement through the
resampling is unreliable:

  * **vertical chroma banding, coherence about 20-30 lines**, row-to-row chroma correlation falling from
    +0.98 at lag 1 to +0.20 at lag 16, with spectral peaks at 48-96 line periods carrying 36-54% of the
    chroma power and **no fixed period**;
  * **a saturated cast**: the mean chroma vector is 73-88% of the mean per-pixel chroma magnitude --
    the frame is almost entirely chroma bias -- at an angle of +21 to +23 degrees, R-B of -0.12 to -0.28;
  * **no horizontal chroma offset**: the chroma-luma correlation is flat from -4 to +4 px.

**Two of my own hypotheses were disproved by those measurements**, which is why they came first:

  * I expected the rainbow to be the subcarrier's per-line phase alternation -- the classic NTSC artefact.
    It is not: adjacent lines correlate at +0.96 to +0.99, so they carry nearly the same colour, and the
    phase drifts smoothly at +0.55 deg/line rather than inverting;
  * I read a bottom border and diagonal edge fringing out of the contact sheet. Both are wrong: no frame has
    a dark bottom run, and the fringing is the saturated cast plus the banding, not a chroma delay. The
    border was the contact sheet's own gap.

**The one mechanism the model lacked** is a recursive vertical chroma filter -- what ntsc-rs calls
`chroma_vert_blend`:

    out[i] = k * out[i-1] + (1 - k) * in[i]

A one-pole IIR down the column, so the influence runs for tens of lines. The existing topologies are all
*symmetric averagers*: `line2` is a three-tap average whose influence dies within two lines and which cannot
drift, so it cannot produce the measured signature. The filter is offered as a sixth separation topology
(`iir`), and it runs **within a frame only** -- ntsc-rs carries `delay` across calls, which couples
consecutive frames, and copying that here would break rendering frames in any order, which everything else
in this renderer depends on.

**And it was unreachable, for a reason worth recording.** With the mechanism implemented, wired, passed and
read correctly, the feedback had no effect on the picture at all. Tracing the call showed the player
receiving `kind='fir2d'` when the schema said 5.0: `separation_kind` was declared with a range of 0..4 and a
value is *clamped* to the declared range during resolution, so the new sixth topology arrived as the fifth.
Everything was right except the schema's upper bound -- the eleventh "reachable but inert" fault in this
project and the second caused by the schema rather than the code. The range now derives from the length of
`YC_SEPARATION`, so adding a seventh cannot leave the two out of step.

Two interface faults on the way there, both raising rather than failing silently, which is the better kind:
`yc_separate` did not accept the new keyword at all, and the parameter name **shadowed the function of the
same name** -- `chroma_vert_blend(c, chroma_vert_blend)` tried to call a float. The parameter is now
`vert_blend` internally and `chroma_vert_blend` in the schema, where it is a user-facing name.

Measured after the fix, vertical chroma coherence at lag 16: `iir` at feedback 0.0 gives +0.243, at 0.6
+0.258, at 0.95 +0.673 -- monotone in the feedback, which is the property the mechanism is for.

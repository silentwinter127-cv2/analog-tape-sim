measure the cell instead of reading the advance table, and add alignment

Two faults, both found by looking at a rendered strip after the numbers had said the layout was right.

**1. The cell was taken from the font's most common advance, which belongs to the wrong script.** 柳宋 and
A-POS declared half-advances of 2400 and 1008 units; measured against what they actually draw, the correct
values are 1200 and 500 -- exactly half. Every Latin character was therefore allotted twice the room it needed,
so a full character's gap followed each one and the text read `婚 礼 录 像 ... K i g u`. Visible in a strip,
invisible in every number I had collected, because the string advance matched the cell by construction: both
were wrong by the same factor.

`hmtx` describes *layout* -- how far to move the pen -- and in these two fonts its most common value belongs to
the CJK glyphs. What a character generator needs is the cell, and the only reliable statement of it is what the
font draws, so the cell is now measured by rendering a probe string and dividing. The full cell comes out at
exactly twice the half cell in all three fonts (2.00, 2.00, 2.02), and a Latin string's ink fills 0.94-0.98 of
its advance instead of 0.74.

**2. Positioning was done by typing spaces, because nothing else was offered.** The user's own project held
`text = "\\n\\n\\n\\n\\n\\n\\n      Kigurumi"` -- seven newlines and six spaces -- to put one word at the bottom
of the frame. That is the work the request was about.

A character generator has a position register: it does not reflow, but it absolutely does place a line, and
OSD chips place lines left, centred or right within the safe area. So `halign` and `valign` are the apparatus's
own controls and need no line breaking. A `pos` convenience sets both at once. The editor gained two dropdowns
and a quick-position list whose default example is *bottom right*, the placement a home recording's OSD usually
has.

**And the readout now answers "where did my text go".** A generator does not wrap, so the commonest reason text
is missing is that it ran off the edge. The first version of this reported the *drawn* width, which is capped
at the frame -- so a line that ran off could never be reported as too wide, which is the one case a person needs
told, and the check that was supposed to catch it passed a line that only fitted by accident. The report now
separates `cells_wanted`, `cells_drawn` and `cells_fit`, and an overflow is decided by the first against the
third. Measured: a line needing 84 half-cells in a 50-cell frame reports `overflow=True` while drawing 52.

Both halves are asserted: the nine positions land within 3 px of where the rule says, a Latin string fills its
advance, and alignment at its default moves nothing -- verified byte-identical against the previous behaviour,
because a new parameter that shifted every saved project would be worse than the fault it fixes.

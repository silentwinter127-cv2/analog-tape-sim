**The lost subtitle was the model working correctly, and the interface said nothing.** The user's preset is
`rental_tape_horror`: `head_switching 0.95` with a 16-line band, `line_jitter 0.35`, `dropout_rate 1.4`. The
titler position writes the text *before* the tape, so the recording damages it -- and the head-switching band
sits at the top of the frame, which is exactly where the subtitle was. Measured: at the camera position the
text's region changes by 0.91 with 2297 pixels of ink; at the titler position 0.11 with 43. The text is gone
because the tape ate it, which is what choosing that position means.

That is a real and intended consequence, and the panel now says so when the position is selected. It is the
second time the user reported something the model was doing deliberately and the interface did not explain.

**Styling, from the user's reference templates.** Those are the classic character-generator look: a filled
glyph in one colour inside a contrasting outline in another -- green inside near-black, red inside near-black,
hard-edged throughout. The piece the model lacked was the **outline's own colour**: it was a fixed near-black,
so green or red text could not be produced at all. Added:

  * `outline_red/green/blue`, because a generator's outline is a separate pen rather than a darkened copy of
    the glyph;
  * `italic`, as a **shear applied to the mask after thresholding**. A bitmap generator had no oblique; it
    sheared the glyph, which is why slanted OSD text has stepped edges. Shearing before thresholding would
    smooth it, which is the opposite of the effect;
  * `shadow_x/shadow_y`, because a drop shadow is offset in both axes and only the strength existed;
  * **six style templates**, one click each, with index 0 meaning custom so the colour parameters stay
    reachable -- a template that could not be turned off would make them unreachable.

**And three fonts already on the machine, which is what the user asked about.** The reference templates are
drawn in a machine-readable face, and the machine has it: **OCR-A BT** and **OCR-B 10 BT**, registered and
measuring 119 and 132 glyphs. Both are Latin only, which is recorded honestly -- Chinese style still needs one
of the two CJK bitmap fonts already there. `Ming-Imperial-1.ttf` is present but unreadable by the loader, and it
reports as unavailable rather than failing.

**Cherry-picked from the CRT request: two faults the tube did not have.** The existing `geometry_warp` is
radial, and a real set has more than that:

  * **a trapezoid raster** (`trapezoid_x`, `trapezoid_y`), because the yoke's scan need not be rectangular to
    the tube face -- and width-taper and height-taper are independent faults, so they are two parameters;
  * **misconvergence** (`convergence`), because three beams leave three guns at different angles and land
    through a shadow mask, so the red and blue images are scaled slightly differently about the centre. **The
    shape is the claim**: the error is zero at the centre and grows outward, which is why a real tube fringes at
    the edges and not in the middle. Measured edge 0.0516 against centre 0.0133, a ratio of 3.89. A uniform
    channel shift would fringe evenly and pass a merely-different test.

Four checks added, all measured on the rendered picture: every template draws and has its own colour, each tube
fault moves the frame, misconvergence has its radial shape, and an outline makes white text legible on a bright
frame.

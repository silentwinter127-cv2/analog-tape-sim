add the tube face's curvature, and rebuild the scanlines from the beam

**The scanlines were hardcoded, and the user's instinct was right.** The code was

    mod = 0.5 + 0.5 * cos(2 * pi * rows / 1.4)
    row = 1 - scanlines * 0.45 * mod

with a comment saying the period of 1.4 "output lines" keeps a fine structure visible. That is a number chosen
for appearance, and the reason it looks fake is deeper than the period: **it darkens whole rows without
reference to the beam**. A real scanline is not a painted dark line, it is the *gap between beam passes*, and
its shape comes from the beam's finite spot.

So the mask is built from a Gaussian spot of a given width in lines, at a given line pitch, and the parameter
that controls it is the spot. **Measured: the line-to-line depth falls from 0.4316 at a spot of 0.20 to 0.0740
at 1.30, monotonically, with the frame's mean unchanged to 0.0005.** That is a focus control: as the spot grows
to the pitch the adjacent passes overlap and the structure disappears, which a cosine of fixed period can never
reproduce.

**It was written inert twice before it worked, and both times for the same reason.** The deviation was
normalised before the depth was applied -- first by its mean, then by its spread -- and the measurement showed a
constant to five decimal places for four spot sizes, 0.14360 every time. The cause is that the deviation is
sampled **once per line**, so a change of spot width only rescales it and dividing by any statistic of it
cancels exactly. The fix was to stop normalising the shape at all: use the beam absolutely, and remove the mean
shift afterwards, as a separate step. Depth from the beam, level from the mean, in that order.

**The fisheye, which is what was actually asked for.** I had added a trapezoid, which is a different fault: the
yoke's scan not being rectangular to the tube face. What the user wanted is the *curved face*, which magnifies
radially -- so the signature is that the centre barely moves while the edges bow. Measured at curvature 0.8:
**centre 0.00204, edge 0.13829, a ratio of 68**. A uniform zoom would change the frame by a comparable total
and fail that check, which is why the check asserts the shape rather than the difference.

Three checks added, all on shape: the curvature is radial, the spot controls the depth monotonically, and the
structure costs no light.

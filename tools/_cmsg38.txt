add the shadow mask and high-voltage breathing, and give the colours a picker

**The shadow mask is why a real tube has vertical structure.** Every coloured dot is a phosphor stripe behind a
hole in a metal grid, and the grid's shadow is visible on the glass. It is not a horizontal-only effect: an
aperture grille is continuous vertical wires, a slot mask is staggered slots, a dot mask is round holes on a
staggered lattice. Three types, because the pattern is what identifies the tube, and the pitch is a property of
the *glass* rather than of the signal.

**It was written correct and reached nothing.** The pattern measured properly on its own -- column standard
deviation 0.667, the exact requested period -- while the rendered frame showed 0.05700 with the mask against
0.05751 without it. The cause was ordering: the mask was applied **before** the beam-defocus lowpass, which is
the one filter guaranteed to erase a three-pixel vertical pattern. Physically the grid's shadow is on the glass,
after the beam has been focused and deflected, so it belongs last with the scanlines and the vignette. That also
puts all three panel artefacts in one place, where the order can be read.

**Breathing was measurable but invisible, and the first test could not have failed.** The anode sags under beam
current, deflection sensitivity rises, and the raster grows -- a *dynamic* fault, so it reads the frame's own
mean rather than a parameter, which makes it the first geometry term that varies per frame. The first scale gave
+0.14% to +1.15%: one pixel at the edge, at the threshold of the noise. And the measurement was worthless for a
different reason -- the feature it tracked spanned the whole frame, and magnifying something that reaches both
edges changes no width. **A test that cannot fail is worse than a failing one.** At 0.12 the travel is +0.10% to
+6.94%, which is the order a real colour tube shows on a dark-to-bright cut.

**A latent fault fixed on the way.** The warp was gated on `geometry_warp > 1e-3`, so a project with curvature
or a trapezoid set and that one parameter at zero had the distortion silently skipped. Every geometry term is
now in the gate.

**The apply button existed; the complaint was still right.** It is `btn.onclick = push` and it was simply below
the visible part of the panel in the screenshot. But the underlying fault is real: the style template, the slant
and the alignment changed nothing until a button elsewhere was pressed, so there was no way to tell whether a
choice had taken effect. They push on change now, as the text area already did.

**And the colours have a picker.** Two colours meant six numeric sliders -- `red`/`green`/`blue` and
`outline_red`/`outline_green`/`outline_blue` -- which is the gap the request for a wider range pointed at. Two
native colour inputs write the same six numbers, so the layer stays the source of truth and the numeric
interface keeps working.

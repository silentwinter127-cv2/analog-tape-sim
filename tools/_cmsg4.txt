check the second capture, and rule out the measurement artefact before calling it a fault

The second capture was supplied hoping for a stable fault with the corrector off, to
calibrate the row-time-base amplitude against. Measuring it, the per-row spread still
varies several-fold between samples -- 0.106 to 1.722 px over 25 fields -- so the same
question arises as for the first: is that a property of the tape, or of the measurement?

**The artefact had to be excluded first, and it is a real candidate.** The tracker only
reports rows where it finds structure to align. A nearly flat frame yields few usable rows,
and the spread of a handful of rows is the tracker's own noise rather than the tape's
behaviour -- so a varying stock of usable rows would look exactly like an intermittent
fault, and a mechanism built on it would be built on nothing. Measured across the 25 fields:

    correlation of spread with picture detail         -0.004
    correlation of spread with number of usable rows  -0.282
    correlation of detail with number of usable rows  -0.007

Neither supports the artefact explanation, so the variation is a property of the material.
A check now reproduces the logic on synthetic fields, where the answer is known: a
detail-rich frame gives 240 usable rows, a nearly flat one gives 0, and a varying stock of
rows therefore cannot be mistaken for varying displacement.

**What the second capture does offer.** Its level is far better behaved than the first: the
median spread is 0.315 px and 16 of 25 fields fall within 0.4x-2.5x of that median, with
the excursions concentrated at 1.0-1.7 px rather than spanning an order of magnitude. So a
*dominant* level is measurable here even though the excursions are real, and calibrating
`row_timebase` should use that median rather than any single field -- which is the opposite
of the mistake made on the first capture, where the ratio of two arbitrary moments was read
as deck behaviour.

Recording what is *not* done: the amplitude is still not calibrated. The median spread and
its step/spread composition are now measured on stable material, but turning 0.315 px into
a `row_timebase` value needs the model's own response measured the same way, and that is
the next step rather than something to assume.

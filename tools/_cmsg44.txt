add twelve fonts, two-axis stretch, letter and line spacing, and a two-way slant

**The fonts.** The panel offered three because only three were registered, and one of those -- `Ming-Imperial-1`
-- points at a file that is not on this machine at all, so it had been reporting "unavailable" since it was
added. Eleven more were measured before being registered, with their coverage recorded rather than assumed:

    Latin only   OCR-A, OCR-A Extended, OCR-B, Consolas, Courier New, Gothic
    with CJK     SimHei, SimSun, MS Mincho, MS Gothic, MingLiU

SimSun is the closest to a period titler, being bitmap-derived and the face Chinese subtitles were actually
authored in; MS Gothic and MS Mincho are the same lineage with real Latin coverage. The OCR faces are what the
blue-screen deck templates are drawn in. **14 of 15 now load**, and the panel says which is which rather than
silently dropping characters -- which it already did, and which is how the missing one was found.

**Two independent axes.** `stretch_x` and `stretch_y` scale the glyph in each direction separately, applied to the
mask by re-indexing so the hard edges survive. Measured on a real run: `stretch_x 1.6` gives +60% width and no
height change, `stretch_y 1.6` gives +59% height and no width change.

**Letter and line spacing** are separate from the cell, because a real generator's cell is fixed and the gap
between characters is a property of how the ROM is clocked out.

**And the slant works both ways**, with a slider instead of a tick box -- a checkbox can only write one direction,
so "left slant" existed in the engine and nowhere in the panel.

**The transform took five attempts and the failures are the interesting part.** Each was a different fault and
each needed a different measurement to see:

  * the mask was scaled but the **advance was not**, so `stretch_x 1.6` grew the ink 12% while every character
    overlapped its neighbour. The pen has to step by the stretched cell;
  * `letter_gap` and `line_gap` were parameters the drawing loop **never read** -- the twelfth and thirteenth
    occurrences of this project's most common defect;
  * the shear had **no room**: the mask is exactly one cell wide, so a shifted row was clipped, losing 31% of the
    ink, asymmetrically. A real generator's cell is wider than its glyph and that margin is where a slant lives;
  * then it pivoted on the **mask's** middle row rather than the ink's, so the whole glyph sat to one side of the
    pivot and the shear became a translation;
  * and the line that did the work was `out[r, src0+off : src1+off] = wide[r, src0:src1]` -- the same column index
    on both sides, so **every pixel stayed where it was**. The row has to move.

**The measurements were wrong as often as the code.** Three centre-of-mass readings in absolute coordinates said
"both directions lean left", and they could not have said anything else: the shear adds padding and the stamp
compensates, so the glyph translates left by an amount proportional to the slant *for either sign*. A measurement
that translates with its subject cannot see a lean. What worked was the top quarter of the inked rows against the
bottom quarter, which is invariant to translation -- and it gave a clean mirror on four characters at once.

The derived shear was then proved on a known mask, printed as characters, before going near the generator. Three
attempts had already gone in as prose arithmetic and come out as a percentage of lost ink.

make the sensor saturate, and fix the white-balance check that was measuring the scene instead of the loop

**The highlight chain could not produce white.** `_roll_off` compressed the sensor's highlights with

    knee + (1 - knee) * (1 - exp(-(lin - knee) / (1 - knee)))

which is **asymptotic** -- it approaches 1.0 and never arrives. Measured, a lamp at twice the reference white left
the step at **0.991**, with 504 pixels at or above 1.0 going in and **zero** coming out, and only `_halation`
afterwards lifting them back. So no pixel in any picture could be pure white, which is why the highlights read as
flat grey and why the reference -- whose whole character is a large blown white core with a halo -- could not be
reproduced.

The white point now follows the range, which is what `dynamic_range` means: `2 ** ((dr - 8) / 8)` is twice the
reference at 8 stops, 1.32x at 5 and 2.83x at 12, so a narrow-range CCD blows out sooner. A rational shoulder is
normalised so that `comp(knee) == knee` and `comp(white) == 1.0` exactly, both points exact rather than fitted.
Measured: a 2.5x source peaks at 1.102 with 182 pixels at or above white, and a 0.45x source is untouched at
0.434.

**The previous attempt failed because it fixed the symptom at the wrong end of the chain.** It held saturation by
clamping at the end of `apply`, which pinned the very pixels the auto-white-balance loop acts on -- the colour
cast stopped being reduced at all. Mapping the order settled it: `_white_balance` and `_expose`, the gain stages,
**already run before `_roll_off`**, so they already act on a value that may exceed white. There was never an
ordering problem, and the hold was solving one that did not exist. Nothing is clamped now.

**And the check that guarded it was measuring the wrong quantity.** It compared `cast(awb[0])` against
`cast(awb[-1])` on a clip whose cast decays on its own:

    loop off   1.2725 -> 1.0140
    loop on    1.2668 -> 1.0493

so the scene supplied the whole 0.2585 of the reduction and the loop contributed **-0.0353** -- it lags a moving
target and overshoots at the end, which is what a first-order loop does. **The old form passed by 0.0005**, the
margin of a check passing on the scene generator's drift. Sixty frames at tau 1.5 s is also two fifths of a time
constant, so it read the first part of a curve.

The replacement asserts the loop's **own contribution** on a cast that cannot drift -- enabled against disabled at
the same frame -- which is the property the docstring already claimed to test. Measured: 1.0857 against 1.0613,
**2.2% of the cast removed against a stationary target, forty times the margin the old form tolerated.** The loop
has bounded authority (it settles at about 8.5% removed and stops, whatever its time constant) because a real
auto-white-balance corrects towards its metering target rather than nulling every scene, so the threshold asserts
a substantial contribution rather than a null.

Three checks added, all measured on the **camera's own output** rather than the final frame: a saturated highlight
reaches white, a normal scene is not lifted, and the white point follows the dynamic range.

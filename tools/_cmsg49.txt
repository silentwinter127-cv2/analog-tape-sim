model the dark rim: the overflow drain pulling the ring outside a blown highlight below the background

**The observation was right and the mechanism was simply absent.** The reference shows its ceiling downlights as a
blown white core **with a dark ring around it**, then back to the ceiling's own light grey. Measured, the model
had only the outward direction:

    ring 0.710 against a ceiling of 0.698     -> the ring sat 0.012 ABOVE the background
    with halation 1.5, ring 0.737             -> higher still

and the profile fell monotonically from the core. **No strength of `halation` or `bloom_amount` could produce a
rim**, because both add light outward and a rim subtracts it -- so the feature was not mis-tuned, it was missing.

**The mechanism.** A photosite whose well overflows has its excess charge removed by the anti-blooming or
overflow-drain structure, and that structure takes the charge from the *neighbours*, pulling them below their own
exposure. The ring just outside a blown highlight is therefore darker than the background. Modelled as the
sensor's saturation mask dilated by a width in **photosites**, minus the mask itself, with a depression that is a
**fraction of each site's own value** rather than a constant subtraction -- so a bright background gets a
proportionally deeper rim and a black one gets none, because the drain removes charge that is there.

Measured, as the difference from having it off:

    drain 0.15   0.02545 deeper at radius 7 px
    drain 0.25   0.04242 deeper at radius 7 px
    drain 0.35   0.05939 deeper at radius 7 px
    drain 0.25, width 4   0.08009 deeper at radius 8 px

monotone in the parameter, landing at the core's edge, and the frame's mean moves by 0.00022 -- a ring, not a
brightness change.

**Two measurement faults on the way, both this project's recurring kind.** The first version of the mask required
a pixel to be *exactly* at the white point, so the ring landed on the lens's own taper and was absorbed by it:
`drain 0.25` produced no visible rim at all. A photosite overflows as it *approaches* full, which is why the drain
structure is specified with a barrier below the well's capacity, so the trigger is 0.9 of the white point -- and
that also puts the ring where the taper is gentle, which is where the reference's rim sits.

And the second version measured `ring min`, the minimum luma in a window. That window contained a dip the drain
had nothing to do with, so **every setting reported the same 0.694 whether the drain was on or off** -- the fifth
time this project has measured a scene rather than an effect. The check measures `off - on`, which isolates it.

The default is 0.0, so no existing preset moves.

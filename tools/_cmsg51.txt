confirm the dispersion chain end to end, and guard it

The user's instruction was to keep the physical basis correct before adding more camera detail, so this verifies
the chain rather than extending it.

**The chain is: the lens sets a radial displacement, and the composite chroma path decides how much of it reaches
the picture.** Both halves are now measured.

At the camera, on a hard edge near the frame's border:

    chroma_aberration 0.0 -> the R-B edge offset is -0.006 px
    chroma_aberration 1.0 -> 2.265 px

Radial by construction -- the resampling scale is constant, so the displacement is `k * a * r` and vanishes at the
centre, which is why four earlier measurements taken at the frame's centre or over a mean of intensities saw
nothing.

And after the format, walking the parameter that controls the survivor:

    chroma 0.25 MHz -> 0.030 px
    chroma 0.50 MHz -> 0.018 px
    chroma 0.75 MHz -> 0.007 px     the default
    chroma 2.00 MHz -> 2.801 px

with the **control** that says this is the chroma path and not the edge being resharpened: `luma_bandwidth` at 3
and 6 MHz leaves the fringe at 0.007 and 0.000 px.

**Not asserted as monotone, because the measurement is not.** 0.030 at 0.25 MHz is larger than 0.007 at 0.75:
at 0.25 the chroma is filtered so heavily that the edge **centroid** is moved by the filter's own phase response,
which is a property of the metric rather than of the dispersion. The claim the physics makes is that a narrow path
suppresses the fringe and a wide one preserves it, and that is what the check asserts -- an assertion of
monotonicity across an arbitrary factor of three in bandwidth would be fitted to where the samples happen to fall.

So the physical basis is verified at both ends of the chain, and the levers are the honest ones: `chroma_aberration`
for how much the lens disperses and `chroma_bandwidth` for how much of it the format can carry. **Nothing was
tuned to make the effect look stronger** -- widening the coefficient or the bandwidth would both change every
other chroma detail in the picture, and that is a decision for the user rather than a fix.

add field curvature and longitudinal chromatic aberration, each asserted by its own signature

**Field curvature** is a simple lens imaging a flat subject onto a *curved* surface, so the plane in focus at the
frame's centre is not the plane in focus at its corners. The result is a picture that is **sharp in the middle and
soft towards the edges at the same focus setting**, which is why a camcorder's corners never match its centre
whatever the aperture. The blur is a function of the **field radius** -- zero at the centre and rising outward,
because the point-spread function grows with the square of the field angle -- implemented as the standard
approximation rather than a per-pixel kernel: blur at the corner radius and cross-fade with a radial weight, which
is exact at both ends and smooth between.

Measured through the chain on a twelve-pixel grid: corner sharpness **0.06473 -> 0.04509** as the parameter goes 0
to 1, with the centre/corner ratio **1.05 -> 1.37**. The centre softens only slightly because the weight there is
0.02 and 0.98 at the corner.

**Longitudinal chromatic aberration** is the wavelengths focusing at different distances along the axis, so at the
sensor plane one is sharp and the others are not. **Nothing moves**: a red edge and a blue edge are at the same
position and both are wider, so the fringes appear *around* a highlight rather than on one side of it. Measured,
at full strength R/G width **2.43** and B/G **2.71**, with the channel positions moving by **0.0000 px**.

**The three aberrations are now asserted by three different quantities**, and that is the point rather than tidiness:

    field curvature       sharpness varies with field radius     position does not enter into it
    longitudinal CA       channel widths differ                  channel positions must NOT differ
    lateral CA            channel positions differ               widths are secondary

A check that only asked whether the picture changed would pass each of them for the others. That is not
hypothetical: **the lateral kind hid as a blur for several turns** precisely because the measurement was of the
wrong quantity, and the fix was to measure a *position* where the effect is a displacement.

**Two measurement faults on the way, both the same mistake in different clothes.** The field-curvature measurement
first used a four-pixel grid, which this format's own 3 MHz luma path has already softened -- so there was no detail
left for a blur to remove and the parameter measured inert. The longitudinal measurement first used a 30-sample
window for an edge that grows from 24 to 74 samples wide, so the centroid slid to the window's edge and reported a
0.6-pixel "displacement" that does not exist; a symmetric blur cannot move a centroid. **A window narrower than its
subject is the sixth form of measuring the wrong thing this project has found.**

Both default to 0.0, so no existing preset moves.

correct the record on `display.saturation`, which is not inert

The previous commit recorded `display.saturation` as the seventeenth inert parameter:

    saturation 0.00  0.25  0.50  0.75  1.00
    mean |R-B| 0.37993 at every setting

**That measurement was of a disabled stage.** The preset it used, `broadcast_1992_weak`, has the display switched
off, and the display's disabled path returns early -- deliberately, so that a disabled stage does not silently
apply its phosphor response. Measured with the display enabled, which is the state a person tuning colour is in:

    saturation 0.00 -> colour spread 0.09389
    saturation 0.25 -> 0.13512
    saturation 0.50 -> 0.17101     the default
    saturation 0.75 -> 0.20223
    saturation 1.00 -> 0.22903

Monotone across the whole range. The parameter works. **The seventeenth inert parameter was not one**, and the
mistake was mine: `self.saturation` is read at `display.py:99` and used at `:411` as
`sat = 0.5 + 1.15 * self.saturation`, which is correct arithmetic that simply does not run when the stage is off.

**This is the seventh measurement in this project that described its configuration rather than the effect**, and it
is the same family as the rest: a four-pixel grid whose detail the format had already removed, a window narrower
than the edge it was measuring, a mask requiring a value exactly at a white point that the lens had already
tapered. The lesson is not new but it keeps being the one that costs the most:

    before measuring a parameter, confirm the stage that reads it is switched on

**So the user's difficulty has one cause, not two.** `format.chroma_bandwidth` at 0.75 MHz -- the recorder's
encoder -- removes the channel separation the lens produces, which is why raising `camera.chroma_aberration` to
maximum shows nothing: the encoder discards it 6.7% of the way through. Both colour controls that a person would
reach for are working; the binding limit is the format.

**And a start was made on the refactor and reverted.** `atsim/codecs/lens.py` was created to move the seven lens
mechanisms out of `CameraStage`, which is 754 lines and 23 methods -- and on writing it, three of the seven bodies
were reproduced **from memory** rather than moved. That is precisely the fault the split is meant to prevent: it
would have changed behaviour in the same operation as rearranging it. The file is deleted. A split has to be a
**verbatim** move, checked by rendering the same frames before and after and comparing them byte for byte, and it
needs a fresh pass rather than the tail of a long one.

The repository is green at 153 checks with nothing half-done in it.

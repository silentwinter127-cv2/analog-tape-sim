record why the user's test image cannot show a coloured highlight edge

**The subject is at the one radius where the effect is defined to be absent.** The image's lamp sits at the centre
of the frame, and lateral chromatic aberration is `k * a * r` -- proportional to the distance from the optical
axis, so **zero at the centre by construction**. Measured on a synthetic lamp, sweeping the parameter from 0 to 1:

    lamp at the frame's centre    R-B spread 0.1135 -> 0.1354    +0.022
    lamp near a corner            R-B spread 0.1119 -> 0.1757    +0.064

The corner responds three times as much, which is the radial term doing exactly what it should. **The model was
right to show no fringe on a centred lamp**, and the reference the user sent earlier -- the ceiling downlights --
showed its fringes on the lights at the *edges* of the frame, which is the same fact seen from the other side.

**But the centre is not the whole story, and one thing is still open.** On the user's own image at maximum
aberration the frame's total colour rose only **0.04398 -> 0.04476**, i.e. 1.8%, across chroma bandwidths from 0.75
to 2.0. Two candidates remain and they were not separated:

  * **the chroma bandwidth**, measured earlier to leave 6.7% of a fringe at 0.75 MHz. A 2.7-pixel displacement
    against a chroma path with about ten pixels of resolution is at the edge of what it can carry;
  * **the subject**: the image's bright areas are a *blown* ceiling light and a soft halo, and a blown area is
    saturated to white with **no chroma left to displace** -- a fringe needs a coloured edge, and the soft grey
    halo has very little.

That the two were not separated is the fault in this pass: the check that would do it -- a hard, coloured edge at
a known radius on the user's own image -- was not run, and the synthetic hard-edge test on a plain background did
show the effect (a fringe going from 0.030 to 2.801 px as the chroma bandwidth widens). **So the chain works and
the open question is which of the two suppresses it on real subjects.**

**What is established and safe to act on:**

  * the effect is **radial**, so it is strongest at the frame's corners and absent at its centre -- a test subject
    has to have its highlight off-centre for this to be visible at all;
  * `camera.chroma_aberration` at 1.0 with the defaults gives a centre lamp +0.022 and a corner lamp +0.064;
  * `format.chroma_bandwidth` at 0.75 MHz -- VHS grade -- is the binding limit on the chroma path, and widening it
    to 1.5 (S-VHS) or 2.0 (near-component) is what lets more through. Measured on a hard edge: **28x** more colour
    at the edge;
  * `display.saturation` works and is worth raising to 0.7-0.8, as the previous correction established.

**The next measurement, not yet taken**: a hard high-contrast edge with colour in it, placed a quarter of the way
from the centre, on the user's own image, swept across both parameters. That separates "the bandwidth ate it" from
"the subject had no chroma to eat", and it is the one experiment that decides whether the coefficient needs to be
larger or the recommendation is simply the bandwidth.

Nothing is half-changed: the project's parameters were restored to `chroma_aberration 0.05`,
`chroma_bandwidth 0.75` for both ends, and the suite is green.

make the starburst's rays thin, and correct the diagnosis that prompted it

**The change is kept; the diagnosis was wrong and is withdrawn.**

**What was changed.** The starburst's ray half-width was `1.5 + 4.0 * a`, where `a` is the **flare strength** -- a
quantity with nothing to do with how wide a ray is -- and a six-blade iris drew `2 * blades` rays rather than
`blades`. Both are now right: the width is in pixels and does not scale with the flare's brightness, and the ray
count is the blade count, because an even-bladed iris's opposite blades give the same line, which is why six blades
show six points and not twelve. Measured, the rays carry **3.2x less ink**: 1790 against 558 over r = 12..200.

**What was wrong.** I claimed twelve 8-px rays "overlap into a disc near the centre", on the strength of an earlier
measurement that found all 24 angular sectors carrying ink at 6 blades and at 8. Comparing the two constructions
directly on the same point source, as plain functions:

    radius    old      new
    15        0.999    1.000
    25        1.000    1.000
    40        1.000    1.000

**Both are already stars at every radius.** The rays never merged. What the "24 of 24 sectors" measurement was
actually describing is the **rendered** frame, which includes the iris **ghost polygon** drawn at
`0.14 * max(h, w)` -- about 56 px here -- plus the lowpass and the rest of the pipeline. The uniform ring it found
was the ghost, not merged rays. **So the change stands on its own merit -- a width should not be scaled by a
brightness, and the ray count should follow the blades -- and not as the repair of the fault I named.**

**And the check I wrote to guard it did not fire.** Reintroducing the original width left it passing at 0.97
contrast, so the statistic could not tell the fault from the fix; a check that passes on both guards nothing. It is
removed rather than adjusted, because the quantity it needs is the **raw ray field** rather than the rendered frame,
and reaching that from `verify.py` means reaching into the stage's internals. That is a decision about what the check
should test, not a threshold to tune.

**Two lessons, both already familiar and both worth the repetition.**

**A new check has to be proved to fire**, and proving it is what caught this one -- the same discipline that showed
the `osdPanels` brace check failed on 28 good functions before it was fixed.

**And a measurement has to be taken where the subject is the only thing present.** The ghost polygon sat at exactly
the radius being sampled, so the statistic was describing the ghost. That is the **eighth** form of measuring the
wrong thing found in this work, in the same family as the four-pixel grid the format had already softened, the
window narrower than the edge it measured, the mask requiring a value the lens had already tapered, and the disabled
stage.

156 checks pass, selftest clean, `lens_flare` inert at its default whatever the blade count.

**The mechanism.** An aperture's diffraction spikes scale with wavelength, so the three channels form theirs at
slightly different angles and lengths and the result is **coloured streaks** -- separate red, green and blue rays
fanning out of a blown highlight. That is what the user's TRV310E reference photographs show, most clearly as a blue
vertical streak running down a lamp post under an orange glow in the fifth one.

**The starburst was achromatic**, which is why no strength could produce it: one ray field computed once and added
with a single blue-violet tint from the coating stack. A single geometry multiplied by a colour gives all three
channels the same rays, so the spikes cannot separate **at any parameter value**. It is now formed per channel, with
each channel getting its own lateral displacement across the spike and its own length, and the parameter is
`spike_spread`.

**Measured on the flare's own output**, at the spike band r = 20..48:

    spike_spread    channel separation
    0.00            0.00000     the control: the geometry is shared by definition
    0.25            0.02384
    0.50            0.05615
    0.75            0.10012
    1.00            0.15597

Red and blue come out almost equally displaced from green, which is the symmetry the physics predicts.

**Two measurements failed before this one, and both are the same mistake.** Three attempts read the separation off
**rendered** frames, and every one of them reported **0.22-0.27 at zero spread** -- where the ray geometry is shared
by definition. The cause is that the renderer carries I and Q at 0.75 MHz against luma's 3 MHz, so the three
channels of a rendered frame differ **by construction**, whatever the flare did. **The statistic was describing the
pipeline.** Called directly on the mechanism's own output, zero spread reads exactly 0.00000.

That is the eleventh instance of measuring the wrong thing in this work and it belongs to the same family as the rest:
the four-pixel grid the format had already softened, the window narrower than the edge it measured, the mask
requiring a value the lens had already tapered, the disabled stage, the ghost polygon at exactly the sampled radius,
and the measurement that translated with its own subject. **The check now asserts the control as well as the effect**
-- bit-identical channels at zero spread -- because that is what proves the statistic is measuring the mechanism.

**And the first version of the effect was too weak, which the control exposed.** The initial shear was
`spread * 0.035` radians, **two degrees**, against 60 degrees between blades on a six-blade iris: two degrees moves a
spike by a fraction of its own width. It is now a lateral displacement across the spike of up to 0.9 px, comparable
to the spike's own width, plus a length difference of up to 22% between red and blue.

**Cost is about 30%** on a frame with the flare enabled, since the ray field is formed three times rather than once;
`lens_flare` at zero skips all of it. 159 checks pass, selftest clean.

**What is not done:** the five reference photographs also show a **vertical blue streak** and hard-edged ring
structures that are not diffraction spikes -- that is per-channel focus, which is the existing `longitudinal_ca` but
applied to the flare rather than the picture, and it is the next mechanism rather than a parameter of this one.

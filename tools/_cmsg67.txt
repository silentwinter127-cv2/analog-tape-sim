defocus the flare per channel, so a bright source wears a coloured halo

**The mechanism the reference material points at.** Longitudinal chromatic aberration puts the three channels at
different focal distances, so the **out-of-focus image of the aperture is larger in blue than in red**. On a bright
source that is a coloured halo *around and below* the light rather than a colour painted on it -- which is what the
five TRV310E photographs and the DSC-W210 video all show: a blue streak under a street lamp, a blue trail down a
lamp post under an orange glow, and hard rings around a blown lamp. It is the same physics as the lens module's
`longitudinal_ca`, applied to the **flare** instead of the picture, and it needs its own parameter because the flare
is light that has already left the aperture's focus.

**Measured as a width, and that is the point.** Three earlier attempts measured colour on **rendered** frames and
every one was dominated by the chroma path -- I/Q at 0.75 MHz against luma's 3 MHz -- so a rendered frame's channels
differ by construction and the statistic described the pipeline. A **width** cannot be confounded that way:

    flare_defocus    red        green      blue       blue/red
    0.00             15.5299    15.4929    15.4281    0.993
    0.50             15.6853    16.0820    16.6175    1.059
    1.00             15.9891    16.8770    17.6996    1.107

Blue widens **10.7%** against red and green sits between them, which is the order the physics requires.

**Two faults had to be fixed to get there.** Applied to the whole flare field the ratio was **1.00** across the
entire range, because the field's second moment is about 61 px and dominated by the achromatic ghost polygon at
56 px -- a 5-17 px kernel moves that by well under a percent. And that placement was wrong physically as well:
defocus acts on the **image of the aperture**, a compact disc around the source, not on a long spike or on a polygon
drawn at a fixed radius. The defocus is now applied to the flare's compact core, with the rest passed through.

**And the check for the spikes caught a real interference.** With `flare_defocus` at its default of 0.5, the spike
check's own control -- that at `spike_spread = 0` the three channels share one ray geometry -- started reading
**0.130** instead of 0, because **two mechanisms were separating the channels at once and the check for one was
reading the other**. Both checks now set the other mechanism to zero explicitly, so each measures only itself. The
control is what caught it: a check that merely asserted "the channels differ" would have passed either way and told
nobody which mechanism did it.

**Also fixed while here:** the per-channel smear from the previous turn was **half converted**. The comment said
"each channel keeps its own column profile" and the line under it was `cols = hot.mean(axis=0)`, which averages the
three channels back into one -- a comment claiming behaviour the code did not have, which is the fault this project
keeps finding. It now keeps the channels separate, with a wavelength-dependent transfer efficiency so that blue
lingers.

161 checks pass, selftest clean. `flare_defocus` defaults to 0.5 and `lens_flare` to 0, so no preset moves.

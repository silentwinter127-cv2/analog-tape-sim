locate the colour drift to the player stage, and record that it is not the highlight mechanisms

**The problem this answers.** Feeding the chain a **clean** modern photograph produced a magenta-and-green banded
frame, and every attempt to demonstrate a highlight effect was drowned in it. The user's correction was the key: the
TRV310E screenshots are **targets**, not inputs, and stacking the model on an already-degraded frame compounds the
artefacts. A clean input shows the drift plainly, which is exactly what makes it measurable.

**Measured, on a clean CC0 source** (snow under sodium light, W.carter, Wikimedia Commons, no blown pixels and no
crushed blacks), compared at a fixed small size so resolution cannot flatter it:

    the source at 120x90      mean RGB [0.7519 0.3592 0.0184]   R-B +0.7336
    the chain at its default  colour drift 0.06399              R-B +0.6505
    the chain at its most     colour drift 0.11450              R-B +0.6288
    neutral

**The most neutral settings drift more, not less**, which rules out every mechanism the schema offers -- they were
all switched off for that row -- and points at the signal path itself.

**And stage by stage, with the display switched off, it is one stage:**

    after stage    channel means                  R-B
    (the source)   [0.7517 0.3586 0.0183]        +0.7334
    camera         [0.8291 0.3693 0.0222]        +0.8069
    format         [0.8291 0.3693 0.0222]        +0.8069     unchanged
    codec          [0.8291 0.3693 0.0222]        +0.8069     unchanged
    connector      [0.8291 0.3693 0.0222]        +0.8069     unchanged
    tape           [0.8291 0.3693 0.0222]        +0.8069     unchanged
    player         [0.7565 0.3681 0.1199]        +0.6365     <- the blue channel rises 5.4x
    display        [0.7565 0.3681 0.1199]        +0.6365     unchanged

**The entire drift happens in one stage, and it is the blue channel that moves** -- from 0.0222 to 0.1199, a factor
of 5.4, while red falls from 0.8291 to 0.7565. A sodium-lit scene has almost no blue in it, so lifting its blue by
that much is what turns a single orange into magenta and cyan bands. The format, codec, connector and tape stages
change the channel means **not at all**, so nothing earlier in the chain contributes.

**The player's chroma decoding is therefore the suspect**, and the reading is consistent with what the stage is set
to: `chroma_bandwidth` 0.75 MHz and `chroma_vertical_averaging` 0.85 are **VHS-grade** numbers, and they are the
defaults. On a saturated single-colour scene a chroma path that coarse bleeds chroma across the frame, and the
raised blue is the bleed. That is the same class of fault as the DV case fixed earlier: **a limit belonging to one
format being applied where it does not apply.**

**What this means for the highlight work.** Every comparison attempted this turn was invalid, because the
artefacts the player introduces on a clean input are **larger than the effects being demonstrated** -- measured,
0.064 of colour drift against the ~0.011 of fringe the chromatic aberration produces. That is why nothing showed,
and it is not a tuning problem in the mechanisms.

**Not fixed, and deliberately.** The player's chroma decoding needs to be examined on its own rather than adjusted
until the picture looks right, because a change there moves every preset and every earlier measurement. The
attribution above is the prerequisite, and it is now done: one stage, one channel, and a factor.

**Tools kept, because each one is what produced a number above:** `tools/fetch_clean.py` (a clean, licensed source
with its provenance), `tools/quantify_drift.py` (the drift and the single-parameter sweep),
`tools/channel_drift.py` (the stage-by-stage attribution), and `tools/render_focused.py`.

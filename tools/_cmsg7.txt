make the head-switching line match what real VHS measures, and fix two things found on the way

The report was that the line is not clearly visible and that VHS should show one by
default. Measuring real material and the model together turned up three separate problems,
none of which was the one I first assumed.

**The band was too short.** On a flat field with the comb neutralised, 10 lines per field
puts the band at 3.5 per cent of the frame height, where real VHS content frames measure 5
to 27 per cent. At 16 lines the model gives 5.2 per cent and at 24 it gives 8.0, so the
default moves to 16. This is what "not visible enough" looks like numerically.

**The band's interior was made of grain.** And the real band is not grainy: measured, its
noise is only 1.1 to 1.4 times the picture's. The old form, `0.045 + 0.055*|noise|`, gave
the interior a per-pixel standard deviation six times the surrounding grain, so the
horizontal discontinuity that actually defines the band was buried under noise real VHS does
not have. The level is now flat with a small ripple, and the edge stays hard.

**Three measurement faults, each of which made the earlier attempts blind.**

Four tuning attempts changed nothing at all, and each had a different cause:

  * the head-switch measurement ran on the synthetic test pattern, where the concentric
    rings' periodic structure registers as noise. Every configuration -- including all tape
    mechanisms switched off -- reported the same rising ramp. It was measuring the content;
  * even on the flat field, the largest row steps sat in the *middle* of the frame, at rows
    270 to 318, not at the bottom. Neutralising the format layer dropped them from 0.0291 to
    0.0004, so the detector had been measuring the comb filter and cross-luminance artefacts,
    which are far stronger than the band and sit somewhere else entirely;
  * and the parameters never arrived. `merge_values` keeps only the keys present in its
    first argument, so a hand-built "quiet" dictionary without `head_switch_lines` in it
    dropped the parameter under test: setting it to 0, 0.45, 0.85 and 1.2 all delivered the
    preset's 10, and seven configurations produced byte-identical readings. That is the
    second time this exact trap has been mistaken for a dead mechanism -- the row-time-base
    calibration was the first.

The measurement tool now asserts that the value it set is the value that arrived.

**A duplicated parameter.** `head_switch_lines` was defined twice, a leftover from rebuilding
the module after it was truncated. The later definition won, so nothing was visibly broken,
but the two had already drifted apart in their help text, meaning a future edit to the wrong
one would have had no effect. Removed, one definition remains.

**And two attempts to remove it deleted far more than intended** -- 77 lines once, an entire
`tracking_error` definition the second time -- because both computed the deletion bounds
arithmetically rather than checking what they were removing. The third attempt asserts the
block contains exactly one `tpe.define`, parses the result before writing it, and confirms
the parameter count is unchanged afterwards: 45 before, 45 after. Line arithmetic on a file
whose exact contents are only approximately known is not a safe way to edit it, and that is
worth recording next to the two failures.

find the encoder/decoder mismatch: the format stage attenuates the chroma it modulates, and the player attenuates it again

**The mechanism, measured on one flat field with nothing else varying.**

    I/Q the encoder was given        I +0.3435   Q -0.0230
    the ideal composite              std 0.1534
    the format stage's real composite std 0.1057        <- 31% of the chroma amplitude is gone

    the player recovers from the ideal composite    I +0.3425  (99.7%)  -> rgb correct
    the player recovers from the real composite     I +0.1638  (48%)    -> blue 0.2264 against 0.0184

**So the encoder attenuates the chroma it has just modulated, and then the player attenuates it again** -- both ends
are set to the same 0.75 MHz, so one loss is applied twice and the recovered chroma amplitude falls to **48%**. A
colour whose chroma is halved does not merely desaturate: the reconstruction redistributes the error across the
channels, which is why the **blue** of a sodium-lit scene is multiplied by 5 while grey and white round trip
exactly.

**And the proof is the control that closes it:** with `format.chroma_bandwidth` raised to 6 MHz the real composite's
std becomes **0.1573**, essentially the ideal 0.1534, and the player recovers **I +0.3187, which is 93%** instead of
48%. So the loss is the encoder's chroma filter acting on the **subcarrier it just created**, and nothing else.

**Why no check caught it.** Every setting swept on a flat field left the output unchanged:
`chroma_bandwidth` from 0.4 to 3.0 changed nothing, nor did the vertical averaging, the luma bandwidth, the comb
depth, the crosstalk terms or the chroma noise. That is not a contradiction -- **a lowpass does not change a
constant**, and a flat field's I and Q *are* constant. The loss appears in the **modulated** signal's amplitude, not
in I and Q, so a measurement that looks at I and Q, or at a flat field's colour, cannot see it. **The quantity that
shows it is the composite's own standard deviation.**

**This is the third fault of the same shape in this work**, and the pattern is now clear enough to name: **a boundary
between two things that were each verified alone.** The DV chroma limit was one path's constraint applied on another;
the `field_curvature` difference came from a copy reordering a float32 operation; and here the encoder and the
decoder each behave correctly against their own idea of a composite and nothing compares the two.

**And it means every highlight comparison of the last two turns was invalid.** The drift this introduces is **0.064
of colour**, against the **~0.011** of fringe the chromatic aberration produces and the same for the flare
defocus's blue lift. Six to one against, which is why nothing showed -- not a matter of tuning the mechanisms.

**The fix, stated but not applied.** The two ends must not both apply the same band limit: either the encoder should
modulate first and band-limit the **composite** (which is what a real recorder does -- the limit is on the channel the
signal is carried in, not on the baseband chroma before it is placed there), or the player's demodulation should
account for the encoder's filter. The first is the physically right one, and it is a change to the **order** of two
operations inside the format stage. It is not applied because it moves every preset and every measurement made
before it, and choosing between those two is a decision rather than a repair.

two attempted fixes for the encoder/decoder mismatch, both measured, both reverted

**The fault, already attributed:** the format stage's composite retains **68.9%** of an ideal one's amplitude, and
the player recovers only **47.7%** of the I the encoder was handed -- which multiplies the blue channel of a
sodium-lit scene by five. Two fixes were attempted and neither works. Both negatives are recorded because both
close off a direction.

**First attempt: band-limit the composite instead of the baseband chroma.** The reasoning was that a real recorder
limits the **composite** -- the signal actually written to tape, whose bandwidth is a property of the medium and the
head -- rather than the baseband colour difference signals before they are placed on a subcarrier. The limit exists
because chroma shares the luma's spectrum, so applying it before the sharing has happened limits something else.

**Measured, it destroys the signal:**

    the composite's mean        0.4378 -> 0.1920
    its std                     0.1534 -> 0.1352
    the player recovers         I = 0.0000          the colour vanishes and the frame goes grey

**The cause is aliasing, and it means the original order is the one that works.** At the working resolution the
pixel rate is below the 3.58 MHz subcarrier, so the composite's subcarrier sits at a **folded** frequency and a
lowpass evaluated in Hz removes it as though it were high-frequency detail. The encoder's order -- limit the
baseband, then modulate -- is what keeps the subcarrier intact. **My reasoning about the ordering was wrong, and the
measurement is what showed it.**

**Second attempt: remove the player's band limit, on the theory that the encoder's limit already models the
recording.** In a real deck the chroma bandwidth is a property of the recording, applied once on the way in, and the
player's job is to demodulate what the tape carries. Measured:

    player as it is              encoder I 0.3435   recovered I 0.1638   47.7%
    player chroma_bandwidth 6    encoder I 0.3435   recovered I 0.1638   47.7%   no change at all
    player separation_kind = 1   encoder I 0.3435   recovered I 0.2013   58.6%

**Widening the player's chroma bandwidth changes nothing**, so its limit is not what costs the other half. **So the
"one limit at each end" model that both attempts were built on is wrong**, and that is worth stating plainly: the
encoder loses 31% and the player then recovers 47.7% *of what remains*, which is **two independent losses and the
second one is not in the bandwidth at all**. `separation_kind = 1` recovers somewhat more, which says the second
loss is in the **demodulation arithmetic** rather than in any filter.

**What is established, and it is more than before:**

    the matrix pair rgb -> Y/I/Q -> rgb is exact (1e-4), and exact on grey and white
    the player's separation is exact against a composite built by composite_encode (0.25%)
    the format stage's composite retains 68.9% of an ideal one's amplitude, and the whole of that is chroma_bandwidth
    the player recovers 47.7% of the encoder's I from that composite, and widening its bandwidth does not change it

**So there are two separate faults, not one**, and the next step is the second: `_separate` is accurate against an
ideal composite and loses more than half against the real one, which points at how the demodulation handles a
composite whose amplitude is already reduced -- a normalisation, a phase reference, or a limiter -- rather than at
any bandwidth.

Both attempts were reverted; `format.py` is byte-identical to its committed state, confirmed by an empty diff, and
the baseline numbers are unchanged (68.9% and 102.6%).

**Tools kept:** `tools/which_op.py` (which chroma operation costs the amplitude -- it isolated `chroma_bandwidth`
and cleared the vertical averaging) and `tools/boundary.py`, `tools/revert_and_probe.py`.

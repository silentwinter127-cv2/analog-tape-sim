the separation is linear, so there is one fault and not two -- correcting the previous conclusion

**Measured, on the same flat field, with the composite's chroma scaled by a known factor:**

    chroma x    composite std    recovered I    fraction of what was put in
    1.000       0.15337          0.34254        0.997
    0.800       0.12269          0.27403        0.997
    0.689       0.10567          0.23604        0.997
    0.500       0.07668          0.17129        0.997
    0.350       0.05368          0.11990        0.997
    0.200       0.03067          0.06853        0.997
    0.100       0.01534          0.03426        0.997

**A constant fraction across a tenfold range**: the separation is **linear in chroma amplitude** to 0.3%. So the
player's demodulation loses nothing. It is not a normalisation against a reference, not a phase estimate degrading
at low amplitude, and not a threshold biting -- **all three would show as a fraction that falls with the scale, and
it does not.**

**And that makes the previous conclusion wrong.** Last turn recorded "two separate losses, and the second one is not
in the bandwidth at all". There is **one** loss, and the second number was simply its consequence:

    the format stage's composite retains  68.9% of an ideal one's amplitude
    the player recovers                   68.9% x 0.997 = 68.7% of the encoder's I

**And the same test shows the colour error is the direct consequence of that amplitude, with no second mechanism:**

    chroma x 1.000  ->  blue 0.0821
    chroma x 0.689  ->  blue 0.1584      the factor the format stage actually retains
    chroma x 0.500  ->  blue 0.2375
    the source          blue 0.0184

**One fault: the encoder's chroma band limit at 0.75 MHz, applied to baseband I/Q before modulation, removes 31% of
the chroma the composite carries.** Everything downstream is faithful to what it is given.

**Which leaves one question and closes the alternatives around it.** The limit itself is correct -- a real recorder
does band-limit chroma, and a narrower chroma bandwidth is exactly what a worse format is. What is wrong is that
**the decoder does not put back what the encoder took**, and the two obvious ways to do that have both been
measured and both fail:

  * limiting the **composite** instead, after modulation: destroys the signal, because at this resolution the pixel
    rate is 11.33 MHz against a 3.58 MHz subcarrier and a Hz-evaluated lowpass on the composite removes the
    subcarrier as though it were high-frequency detail. Measured, the recovered I went to **0.0000**;
  * removing the **player's** own limit: changes nothing at all. Measured, `chroma_bandwidth` 0.75 -> 6 MHz left the
    recovered I at **0.1638**.

**So the fault is a genuine encoder/decoder asymmetry in what the band limit means**, not an ordering error and not a
duplicated filter. The encoder's limit is a *recording* property and the decoder's is a *reception* property, and the
model applies the first without the decoder accounting for it. The right shape of the fix is therefore that the
player's separation must know the width the recording was limited to -- which it does, as `self.chroma_bw` is already
available to it, and it uses that value for its own filter rather than for the reconstruction.

**What is fixed in place now, so the next attempt starts from a number:** an ideal composite round trips at 0.997;
the format stage's retains 68.9%; and the target for any fix is that the player's recovered I should be **0.3435**
against an encoder input of 0.3435, from the format stage's real composite.

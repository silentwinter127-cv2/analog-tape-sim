locate the chromatic banding to `_separate`, with three suspects inside it ruled out

**The fault.** On a clean, smooth, single-colour sodium-lit source, with every highlight mechanism at zero, the chain
raises the row-to-row R-B variation from **0.00342 to 0.01171** and the blown area from 4.3% to 26.1%. That artefact
is larger than any highlight effect the model produces, which is why several turns of comparisons showed nothing.
Until it is fixed, no effect can be exhibited or judged.

**Attributed stage by stage**, carrying one frame through by hand and printing the statistic after each:

    after stage    colour banding   change      blown
    (the source)   0.00342                      0.04258
    camera         0.00190          -0.00152    0.05822
    format         0.00190          unchanged   0.05822
    codec          0.00190          unchanged   0.05822
    connector      0.00190          unchanged   0.05822
    tape           0.00190          unchanged   0.05822
    player         0.01389          +0.01199    0.20322     <- all of it
    display        0.01389          unchanged   0.20322
    overlay        0.01389          unchanged   0.20322

**And inside the player, three suspects excluded by measurement rather than by reading:**

**The chroma band limit.** Predicted two-sided: the banding should grow as the bandwidth narrows and vanish as it
widens. Measured, the player's `chroma_bandwidth` from **0.40 to 12 MHz leaves it at 0.01171, unchanged** -- and the
format's moves it only from 0.01340 to 0.01171. So no filter is responsible. The prediction failed and is recorded as
failed.

**The per-line chroma phase error.** A per-line rotation of a constant chroma vector is exactly a per-line colour
change, so this was the leading candidate: it is driven by `color_noise`, and bypassing `_chroma_phase_error`
entirely should have removed the banding. Measured, with `color_noise` at 0, 0.25, 0.60 and 1.00, and with the phase
step bypassed in every combination -- **0.01171 throughout**.

**The luma frequency response.** A per-row *luma* change becomes a per-row *colour* change through the reconstruction
matrix, whose R, G and B coefficients differ, which is the shape of the fault. Bypassed: **0.01135**, a change of
0.0004.

**What that leaves, and the measurement that proves the location.** With `_separate` replaced by a pass-through that
takes the source's own Y/I/Q:

    colour banding   0.01171 -> 0.00342      which is the source's own 0.00342
    blown            0.26104 -> 0.06285

**Everything except the reconstruction is now excluded, and `_separate` is where the chroma that the source does not
have comes from.** What remains inside it, unfixed and not yet separated from one another:

    yc_separate(...)   twice, the second with the receiver's comb and vertical blend
    demod_chroma(...)  the demodulation arithmetic
    remod_chroma(...)  the re-modulation used to rebuild the composite for the second split
    horizontal_shift   a geometric displacement, which cannot create chroma

**The next step is concrete**: sweep the second `yc_separate`'s `comb_depth` and `vert_blend` -- both are arguments
the player already passes and both are per-row operations on chroma, which is the signature being chased. `comb_depth`
was set to zero in this configuration, so `vert_blend` is the one to try first.

Nothing was changed by this turn: `player.py` is byte-identical to its committed state, and 163 checks pass.

find the invented chroma: `split_composite` returns 40% of a signal a constant field does not contain

**The mechanism, measured on a constant field.** `yc_separate`'s `kind` is `"band"` for this preset, which
dispatches straight to `split_composite(comp, std, comb_depth=0, chroma_bandwidth_hz=...)`. That function is one
idea in four lines:

    chroma = fft_bandpass(comp, fsc - 1.6 * half, fsc + 1.6 * half, std, unity_at_hz=fsc)
    luma   = comp - chroma

A **constant** field has no energy at 3.58 MHz, and its encoded I/Q is exactly zero, so the bandpass must return
nothing. Measured:

    width   rows    chroma returned   as a fraction of the I the field was encoded with
    320     240     0.13790           0.401
    720     480     0.13798           0.402
    1280    720     0.13801           0.402
    1920    1080    0.13799           0.402
    3840    2160    0.13781           0.401

**Forty per cent, at every resolution from 320 to 3840.** And the same figure, 0.1379, is what the real picture
produces -- so this is not a property of the photograph, it is the separator generating chroma from nothing and then
subtracting it from luma, which is why a smooth sodium-lit scene acquires magenta and green bands and 26% blown area
where the source has 4%.

**The bandpass' own DC response is exactly zero**, so this is not a DC leak. It is passing energy *at a frequency*.
The arithmetic that matters: the pixel rate at every one of those widths is **11.33 MHz** against a subcarrier of
**3.58 MHz** -- about **3.17 samples per cycle**, with the subcarrier at 63% of Nyquist. That is the same class of
problem as the composite-domain lowpass fixed earlier, which removed the subcarrier entirely because a filter
evaluated in Hz against an FFT behaves according to where the subcarrier lands among the bins, not according to the
bandwidth it was asked for.

**And one parameter the player passes is never read on this path.** I intended to sweep `vert_blend`, since the
vertical chroma blend is a per-row chroma operation and that is the signature being chased. It is unreachable:
`yc_separate` only calls `chroma_vert_blend` in the `box`, `line1`, `line2`, `fir2d` and `iir` branches, and the
`band` branch -- which this preset selects -- returns before reaching it. So `player.chroma_vert_blend` is a
parameter that is read, stored, passed and **ignored**, which is worth recording separately from the banding.

**Three hypotheses were proposed and refuted before this one, and each refutation is a result:**

  * the chroma band limit -- player `chroma_bandwidth` from 0.40 to 12 MHz leaves the banding at 0.01171 unchanged;
  * the per-line chroma phase error -- bypassed entirely, and `color_noise` at 0, 0.25, 0.60, 1.00, all 0.01171;
  * the luma frequency response -- bypassed, 0.01135 against 0.01171.

**and the location is fixed by replacing `_separate` with a pass-through:** colour banding 0.01171 -> **0.00342**,
which is the source's own value, and blown 0.261 -> 0.063.

**Not fixed.** The question the fix has to answer is what `split_composite` should return for a field with no chroma,
and the honest answer is not "widen the band" -- measured, widening it changes almost nothing because the problem is
where the subcarrier sits in the sampled spectrum, not how wide the passband is. The candidates are a bandpass that
respects the sampled subcarrier's actual position rather than being evaluated as an ideal analogue filter, or
building the composite at a rate where the subcarrier is not near Nyquist. **The first is a change to
`fft_bandpass`'s notion of frequency and would move every composite measurement in the project; it needs its own
round and its own before/after evidence.**

Nothing was changed: `signal.py` and `player.py` are byte-identical to their committed state, 163 checks pass.

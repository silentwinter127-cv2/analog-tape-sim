add tape-deck stiction: the first intermittent mechanism in the model

Measured on a real capture, the per-row displacement spread flips between roughly 0.1 and 3
pixels from one sample to the next -- a factor of fourteen within seconds, and uncorrelated
with picture content. That is the shape of an *intermittent* fault, and it was the one kind
the model had no representation for: every existing mechanism scales smoothly with a
parameter, so none of them can produce it. Smoothing a per-frame field to look like a fault
removes exactly the discontinuities that define one.

The physics is stiction. A tape's binder is hygroscopic, so a cassette stored damp carries a
thin water film; against the capstan and pinch roller that film is adhesive. The tape sticks,
the transport keeps pulling, tension rises until the adhesion gives, and the tape jerks
forward. Then it sticks again. That gives four properties, and each is in the model:

  * **episodic** -- most frames are untouched, a few are far from normal. This is the whole
    character of the fault and the one thing a smoothly-scaling parameter cannot express;
  * each event is a **grab and a release**, so the displacement ramps during the grab and
    snaps back on release. A sawtooth, not a step -- a step would look like a splice -- and
    not a sine, which would look like flutter;
  * it is **position-dependent along the tape**, because the water film and the binder's
    condition are not uniform, which is what `stiction_stretch` sets;
  * it is **temperature- and humidity-dependent in the long run**, modelled as the event
    rate rather than as anything per-frame. That is why the same cassette can be fine in the
    morning and unusable in the evening.

Implemented as events on the timeline, like dropouts and creases, rather than as a per-frame
field. Planned in one pass rather than grown in blocks: the arrivals come from a Poisson
process over the whole clip, so growing it block by block would make each block independent
of the last and the arrival times would depend on how the renderer happened to seek, which
would break the guarantee that the same project produces the same frames.

Measured, at rate 2.0 over 24 frames: 7 frames affected, the largest moving the picture by
0.1378 and the median frame by 0.0000. The affected run ramps 0.0404, 0.0740, 0.1053, 0.1378
and then falls back 0.1247, 0.0866, 0.0476 -- one grab, building and releasing.

Two checks added, and the second is the one that matters: that the events appear at the set
rate, and that the *effect* is concentrated. A check written only against the mean would pass
a mechanism that moved every frame by a similar amount, which would be a displacement field
rather than an intermittent fault.

**A trap caught in the act.** The test helper first built its parameter dictionary as
`merge_values(QUIET, over)`, and `merge_values` keeps only the keys already present in its
first argument -- so `stiction_rate` and `stiction_amount` were silently dropped and the
sweep would have reported a mechanism that does nothing. The assertion added during the
row-time-base work caught it immediately: "a sweep parameter was dropped by the merge". That
is now twice this trap has been mistaken for a dead mechanism, and the assertion is the
reason it was not a third time.

**And a performance mistake worth recording.** The first version of the demo built a renderer
per frame per panel and called `_build_chain()` each time, so it spent its life rebuilding the
chain instead of rendering and had to be killed. Building each renderer once and reusing it
through `render_frame(index)` is both far faster and more correct, since the timeline is
planned deterministically from the seed.

66 checks pass. The visible test is `tools/stiction_demo.py`: a comparison sheet at the
largest grab, a four-panel video across the amount range, and a video of the fault inside the
1992 preset.

build a known-answer instrument for the chroma recovery, and take the first graded reading

**The reference figure that was missing.** Five hypotheses were refuted in turn, and none of them could settle the
question because every measurement compared the recovery against **another measurement** -- the encoder's own ripple,
the source's banding, the picture after the chain. With a **designed** signal there is nothing to compare: the answer
is known by construction, and any row-to-row variation is error.

    Y      constant, so nothing in the luma can masquerade as chroma
    I      a horizontal ramp, constant down every column
    Q      zero

so a correct recovery is row-constant and its ripple is **exactly zero**. Measured, at two strengths:

    I constant everywhere        recovered mean 0.299717 against a designed 0.30; ripple 0.000649
    I a ramp                     mean column error 0.003856; max per-row error 0.006037, which is 0.755% of the
                                 designed range

**So the recovery is accurate, and its error floor is 0.000649.** On the photograph the recovered I ripples
**0.006990** -- **10.8x that floor** -- which makes the photograph's figure real error rather than signal, for the
first time on a quantitative basis.

**And the first graded reading, on the known-answer signal where the correct value is zero at every step:**

    step                                   I ripple    Q ripple
    first yc_separate + demod              0.001159    0.001169
    horizontal_shift(disp)                 0.002104    0.001156      <- where it doubles
    remod + second yc_separate             0.002104    0.001156      <- adds nothing
    demodulating the second split          0.004689    0.003855

    the stage's own _separate returns      0.001842    0.001160

**The second `yc_separate` contributes nothing of its own** -- its `chroma_band` ripple is 0.000192 against the first
split's 0.000075, i.e. lower. **What doubles the figure is applying the demodulation again**, and separately the
`horizontal_shift`. That is a sharper statement than any of the five refuted hypotheses, and it is the first one
supported by a value that is correct rather than merely different.

**The instrument is kept** (`tools/known_answer.py`, `tools/instrument_separate.py`): a designed signal whose answer
is known, and a step-by-step replay that prints the error after each operation. It is what the next attempt should
start from, because it converts "this looks banded" into "this step contributes this much error against a correct
value of zero".

**Nothing changed**: `signal.py`, `player.py` and `standards.py` are byte-identical to their committed state, and
163 checks pass. The reading above is a measurement of the existing code, not of a modification.

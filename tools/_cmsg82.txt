Withdraw the ramp as a criterion: `horizontal_shift` is exact, and the ramp could never satisfy it.

**Two results from testing the operation directly**, which is not what the earlier time-base test did -- that one
zeroed the *source* of the displacement and left the *operation* in place.

**First, `horizontal_shift` is exact.** A constant plane comes back unchanged to `0.000000` at displacements of
0.0, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, -0.5, 1.0, 1.5 **and** under a per-row displacement of standard deviation up to 2
samples. It is not a source of error.

**Second, and this retracts a criterion I had been using.** On a **ramp**, the same operation changes each row's
mean by `d * slope`. **That is what displacing a row does, not a fault.** And `_separate` contains a per-row
displacement, so a signal designed to be **row-constant** cannot remain row-constant through it. **The known-answer
signal was impossible to keep, by construction, and every ripple figure taken from it is confounded** -- the ladder
0.001159 -> 0.002104 -> 0.004689 is part displacement doing its job and part error, in a proportion the statistic
cannot separate.

**It also explains three earlier anomalies at once.**

  * forcing `_timebase_offset` to zero moved the photograph's ripple by only 3%, which read as the displacement being
    innocent -- it was neither; zeroing one term of a sum the statistic cannot break apart;
  * the withdrawn attribution to `_separate` came from a pass-through that also removed the displacement, so that
    observation could never have identified `_separate`'s internals;
  * and the five refuted hypotheses each removed one term of that same sum and each left the rest.

**The signal that avoids the entanglement is a constant**, and it is strictly simpler than the ramp: a constant must
come back constant under a shift, a filter, a demodulation and a re-modulation alike, so **any variation in the
output is error and there is nothing else it could be.** That is the criterion to carry forward.

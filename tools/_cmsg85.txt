find that the chain multiplies horizontal banding by 40-100x at every scale, independent of resolution

**The measurement, on the clean source, with every mechanism at zero.**

    the source file at its native 4363x3279:   R-B banding 0.00100,   luma banding 0.00097

    the source resized to each render scale, BEFORE the chain sees it:
        scale 0.15   654 px    R-B 0.00260   (2.6x the file)
        scale 0.30  1308 px    R-B 0.00196   (2.0x)
        scale 0.55  2399 px    R-B 0.00143   (1.4x)
        scale 1.00  4363 px    R-B 0.00100   (1.0x, no resampling at all)

    and through the chain at each scale, mechanisms at zero:
        scale 0.15             R-B 0.04397   (43.8x the file)   luma 0.09869
        scale 0.30             R-B 0.03957   (39.4x)            luma 0.09812
        scale 0.55             R-B 0.04090   (40.7x)            luma 0.10494
        scale 1.00             R-B 0.03854   (38.4x)            luma 0.10891

**Two things follow, and both are new.**

**It is not the resampling.** That was the candidate left unexamined for seven attempts, and it is refuted: resizing
accounts for at most **2.6x** and *falls* as the scale rises, while the chain's contribution is **38-44x and flat
across the whole range** -- including at scale 1.00, where the source is rendered at its own resolution and is never
resampled at all. So the banding is a property of the chain, not of the input path.

**And it is not resolution-dependent, which makes it a fixed property of the signal path.** Forty times at 654 px and
thirty-eight times at 4363 px, on a picture whose own banding differs by 2.6x between those sizes: the chain is
imposing the same banding regardless of what it is given.

**And luma is affected too -- a hundredfold -- which contradicts what I measured earlier.** At 320x240 the chain left
luma unchanged (0.002631 -> 0.002569) and only chroma rose. Here luma rises from 0.00097 to about 0.10 at every scale.
**One of those two readings is describing something different from the other**, and I do not yet know which; the
difference between the two setups is the render width, the source's own size, and the downsampling path, so the
earlier "luma is clean" conclusion should be treated as **not established** rather than as a finding.

**What this means for the reproduction, stated plainly.** The effect cannot be shown while this is present: at 40x the
artefact, every comparison between a control and an effect is measuring the artefact. **The reproduction was built and
its settings file written** -- a clean CC0 source, the display's gamma set to 1.0 on measurement, the mechanisms at
their strongest, exposure pulled down because the chain brightens the picture 45% on its own -- **and its control and
its effect differ by about 1% in every statistic, which is what the banding does to a comparison.**

**The one useful thing the reproduction attempt produced**: with the display linear and the exposure compensated, the
result has **no blown pixels** (0.0000, against 26% in the previous attempt) and a sensible mean -- so the
exposure/gamma end of the chain is now in a state where an effect could be judged, once the banding is understood.

Nothing changed: the modules are byte-identical to their committed state and 163 checks pass. The banding is a
measurement of the existing code, and it is now known to be (a) real, (b) 40x, (c) resolution-independent, and (d) not
the resampling.

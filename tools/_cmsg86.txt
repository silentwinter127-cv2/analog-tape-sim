measure the banding's shape: a per-row offset in chroma, not a spatial frequency and not the subcarrier

**Two measurements, and the second one names the fault's form.**

**The spectrum says it is not a frequency pattern.** The subcarrier sits at **0.3160 cycles per pixel** (3.165 samples
per cycle), and if the banding came from it there would be a peak there. The strongest components of the rendered
frame are at **0.0008, 0.0084, 0.0161, 0.0229 cycles/px** -- and the **source's own spectrum is almost identical**,
with the same four peaks in the same order and only their shares differing. So the banding carries **no frequency
signature of its own**; it is not a pattern imposed along the row.

**And the row-and-column statistics name its form:**

                        std of the ROW means   std of the COLUMN means
    the source          0.05142                0.05104
    through the chain   0.12277                0.04413

**The source is symmetric** -- 0.0514 against 0.0510 -- which is what a picture's own two-dimensional structure gives.
**Through the chain the row means move by 2.4x while the column means do not move at all.**

**That is the signature of a per-row additive offset**, and it is worth stating why precisely:

  * if the chain had changed the picture's *contrast* in one direction, both statistics would move together, because
    scaling a plane scales every moment of it;
  * if it had imposed a *spatial pattern*, the spectrum would show it, and the spectrum is unchanged;
  * an offset that takes a different value on each row -- and the same value along the row -- raises the spread of the
    row means and leaves the spread of the column means alone. **That is exactly what is measured.**

**So the fault is a per-row offset in the chroma plane**, in R-B specifically, with a magnitude of about 0.07 in the
row means' standard deviation (0.12277 against 0.05142). That is a much narrower statement than any of the seven
hypotheses it took to get here, and unlike them it is derived from the shape of the output rather than from a guess
about a stage.

**And it is consistent with everything measured before**: a per-row offset is invisible to a constant signal (which
has no row structure to be offset), invisible to a vertical pattern shifted sideways within a row, and it survives
every mechanism being switched off -- which is why seven stage-level hypotheses were refuted while the artefact
remained.

**Next, and it is specific**: a per-row offset in a colour-difference plane comes from something that computes one
number per row and adds it. In the player that is the **burst amplitude or phase per line**, in the format the
**line phase process** (`self._line_phase`, which is advanced once per line), and in the tape the **head-switching and
row-error terms** -- all of which are per-row by construction and none of which have been tested on this signal.

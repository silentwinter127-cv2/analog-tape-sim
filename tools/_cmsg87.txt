name the fault: it is the tape's head-switching band, in the bottom 32 rows

**The attribution, measured by the pair of statistics that defines the fault's shape.**

    the source                 row-mean std 0.05141   column-mean std 0.05086   ratio 1.011
    through the neutral chain  row-mean std 0.12851   column-mean std 0.03840   ratio 3.347

The source is **symmetric**, which is what a picture's own two-dimensional structure gives. The chain makes the row
spread 2.5x while leaving the column spread unchanged -- the signature of a **per-row additive offset** -- and one
parameter accounts for it:

    as it is                       0.12851 / 0.03840   ratio 3.347
    player burst_error_deg = 0     0.12851 / 0.03840   no change
    player tbc_residual = 0        0.12851 / 0.03840   no change
    player tbc_enabled = 0         0.12851 / 0.03840   no change
    tape row_chroma_* = 0          0.12851 / 0.03840   no change
    **tape head_switching = 0      0.04918 / 0.03973   ratio 1.238**

**Setting one parameter to zero returns the row spread to the source's own value of 0.05141**, and four other
candidates measured in the same run move neither statistic by a digit.

**And the extent is exactly the band, not the frame.**

    head switching 0.45 -> row-mean std 0.08971
    head switching 0.00 -> row-mean std 0.04918
    the rows it moves most: 224, 231, 227, 232, 239, 238, 217, 220, 225, 237, 236, 235, 234, 208, 230, 233

**every one of them in the bottom fifth of a 240-row frame**, 32 rows carry a deviation above 0.01, and the top 20
rows carry 84% of the total. The parameters are `head_switching` (default **0.85**, range 0..1.2),
`head_switch_lines` (default **16**) and `head_switch_mid_line` (default **0.25**) -- so the band is meant to be about
16 lines at three-quarters of the way down, and at 240 rows that is 32 lines of real disruption.

**Why eight hypotheses missed it, and the reason is worth recording.** Head switching lives in the **`tape` layer**,
and the series of measurements either switched the tape off, passed it an empty configuration, or ran with the tape's
own defaults while every *other* layer was neutralised. **The two hypotheses that did touch the tape examined
`row_chroma_*`, which is zero by default** -- so the term was inert in every configuration tested and its exclusion
was correct but irrelevant. **The fault was in the one layer the measurements were routing around.**

**What this means for the reproduction, and it changes the picture.** The banding is not a defect in the chroma path
at all: it is head switching, a **modelled tape defect**, doing what it is designed to do -- and it is doing it at
**0.85 by default**, which is more than two-thirds of the parameter's range. On a smooth night scene, 32 lines of
disrupted chroma at the bottom of every frame **is** the striped appearance that has been chased for eight attempts.

**So the reproduction's settings file should carry a head-switching value appropriate to the material**, and the
statement that "the chain multiplies banding by 40x" should be withdrawn: the chain was faithfully applying a defect
that was switched on, to material that does not have it.

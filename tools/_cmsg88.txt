reproduce the highlight effect, and find two faults on the way: the head-switching band and the upload contract

**The effect is reproduced.** A blown street lamp with a warm white core and a **teal-blue vertical trail** dragged
down from it, on a dark night frame -- the signature the DSC-W210 clip and the TRV310E photographs show. Measured
against a control rendered on the identical path with all nine highlight mechanisms at zero:

    control (mechanisms zero)  mean [0.0219 0.0437 0.0489]  blown 0.00488  glow 0.01012  row_std 0.02725
    effect  (mechanisms on)    mean [0.0239 0.0462 0.0529]  blown 0.00595  glow 0.01101  row_std 0.02863

The settings file is `tools/ntscrs_ref/repro-highlight.json` -- **24 parameters across 6 layers**, with a fixed seed
and the NTSC standard, so importing it gives the same state rather than a description of one.

**And two faults were found getting there, both of which had been masking the work.**

**The 40x "banding" was the tape's head-switching band, not a defect in the chroma path.** Every one of the eight
measured hypotheses had been routed around the layer it lives in -- the tape was switched off, or given an empty
configuration, or left at its defaults while every other layer was neutralised. One parameter settled it:
`tape.head_switching` at its default **0.85**, out of a 0..1.2 range, puts a per-row chroma offset on the **bottom 32
rows of a 240-row frame**; setting it to zero returns the row-mean spread to the source's own value (0.12851 ->
0.04918 against a source of 0.05141, and the row/column ratio from 3.35 to 1.24). **It is a modelled defect doing its
job**, and on this material it does not belong: head switching is a helical-scan artefact intrinsic to **VHS** and
absent from **MiniDV**, and the reference is a digital-component camera. So the reproduction sets it to 0.0 and says
why.

**And `/api/upload` does not parse multipart.** It reads the raw body with the name in an `X-Filename` header:

    n = int(self.headers.get("Content-Length") or 0)
    raw_name = self.headers.get("X-Filename") or "clip.mp4"

while every upload in this series sent a multipart envelope. **The server has therefore been storing the envelope as
the file**, and the picture arrived only because a JPEG decoder skips to the first valid header. Each side is correct
by itself and the pair was never compared -- the same shape as the DV chroma limit, the `field_curvature` float
reorder and the composite band limit found earlier. The still upload is now sent the way the endpoint reads it.

**And one thing the numbers exposed about the input**, which is worth stating because it invalidated several attempts
at once: **the sodium-lit snow scene has no blown pixels at all** (`blown 0.0000`). Every mechanism under test is a
*highlight* mechanism -- halation is the bloom of a saturated photosite, the smear is charge spilling from one, the
ring is the anti-blooming drain pulling its neighbours down -- so on that frame they were **correctly inert**, and no
value of any of them could produce a visible change. The effect needed a source with a blown light in it, which is
what the DSC-W210 frame provides: `blown 0.005`, `glow 0.010`, mean luma 0.047.

**What the reproduction carries**: the display linear at `gamma 1.0` (measured as the largest single contributor to
the colour drift on a clean source, 0.264 against 0.110), the digital-component path, the band off, the chroma
crosstalk terms at zero because the scene has no chroma detail to misplace, and the nine highlight mechanisms at a
strength where they are visible -- `halation 0.90`, `ccd_smear 0.85`, `flare_defocus 1.00`, `lens_flare 0.55`,
`spike_spread 0.85`, `chroma_aberration 0.85`, `axis_offset 0.45`, `longitudinal_ca 0.65`, `overflow_drain 0.35`.

163 checks pass, selftest clean, and nothing in the model was changed: the reproduction is a settings file.

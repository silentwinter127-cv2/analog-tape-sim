**Reducing the glow lowered the pole's contrast rather than raising it**, which refutes my guess that the softening
mechanisms were eroding the line. The cause is arithmetic: the pole's contrast is its brightness **minus its
surroundings'**, and a glow added to the picture **raises both**, compressing the difference. Turning the mechanisms
down only darkens the whole frame and the ratio barely moves. **And the blue is removed by the same addition**, because
the glow contributes **neutral** light on top of a small chroma difference -- which is why raising the display
saturation does not bring it back: saturation scales an existing difference, it cannot restore one that has been
diluted by neutral light.

**So the honest position.** Both features now appear on a subject that supports them; the pole's blue is the one
quantity that does not survive, for an identified reason (`source_glow` and `halation` add neutral light), and the
value that best preserves the line's contrast is the one written out. `tools/ntscrs_ref/repro-highlight.json` carries
it: 36 parameters across 6 layers, the raster group at zero, `source_glow 0.05`, `halation 0.25`, `ccd_smear 0.20`,
`flare_defocus 0.35`, `lens_flare 0.30`, `spike_spread 0.40`, `overflow_drain 0.60`, `chroma_aberration 0.6`,
`longitudinal_ca 0.4`, `axis_offset 0.5`, and the scene itself kept beside it as `refscene.png` for re-measurement.

**And the fix for the blue, if it is wanted, is a change to the model rather than to the values**: the glow terms add
their light with the highlight's own colour, but `halation`'s tint is **fixed and cool** and its contribution to a
small chroma difference is what neutralises it. Making `halation` inherit the local source colour, as `source_glow`
does, is the structural change that would let a pole keep its blue -- and that is a mechanism to add, not a value to
find.

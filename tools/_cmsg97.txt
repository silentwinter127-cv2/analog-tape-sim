make `halation` carry the source's colour, which is the mechanism that was dividing a thin line's blue away

**The line that did it, found by reading rather than guessing.** `CameraStage._halation` finished with

    tint = np.array([1.05, 0.88, 1.05])
    return rgb + (a * 0.52) * halo[..., None] * tint[None, None, :]

so the halo was built from a **luminance** mask -- colourless by construction -- and then given a **fixed**
magenta-leaning tint. That is a real lens property, but as a **constant** it adds neutral-to-magenta light on top of a
small chroma difference and **divides that difference away**: a bright thin line with its own blue keeps its
brightness, keeps its contrast, and loses its colour. Measured on the reference scene, the pole's hue came back at
**+0.000** against its own **+0.024**, and no value of any existing parameter recovered it -- raising the display
saturation cannot restore a difference that neutral light has divided out.

**The physical fix is the one `source_glow` already uses**: light scattered from a bright source keeps that source's
spectrum. So the halo's tint is now taken from the **blown region itself** -- `rgb * hot`, averaged per channel and
normalised so its **luminance is unchanged** -- which means a scene with no blown source, or a neutral one, behaves
exactly as before and only a **coloured** source moves.

**And the change is opt-in, which is verified rather than asserted.** A new parameter `halation_source_colour`
(default **0.0**) blends between the fixed lens tint and the source's own. At 0.0 the output is **bit-identical** to
the old body, checked with `np.array_equal` on four different sources:

    blue-white (0.75, 0.92, 1.00)     new(0.0) identical to the old body: True
    white (1.00, 1.00, 1.00)          True
    warm-bright (1.00, 0.85, 0.55)    True
    sodium (1.00, 0.72, 0.30)         True

**And at 1.0 the colour follows the source, in both directions:**

    the blown source                 its own B-R   halation 0.0   halation 1.0   follows
    blue-white                       +0.2500       +0.00000       +0.00359       yes
    white                             0.0000       +0.00000       +0.00000       yes (stays neutral)
    warm-bright                      -0.4500       +0.00000       -0.00676       yes
    sodium                           -0.7000       +0.00000       -0.00940       yes

**One test of mine was wrong and is recorded.** The first attempt used a warm patch of (1.00, 0.62, 0.25) and got no
effect at all. The mask is `hot = clip((lum - 0.72) / 0.28, 0, None)`, so that patch's luma of **0.6914** is **below
the threshold** and the mechanism correctly did nothing. **A mechanism that never fires cannot be judged**, and the
source has to be bright -- which a blown lamp is. With the patch above the threshold the effect appears in every case.

**What this does and does not do.** It makes the **colour** of a halo follow its source, which is the property that was
missing and which fixes the *mechanism* rather than the values. It does not by itself restore the pole's blue in the
final render, because the halo around a thin bright line is dominated by the line's own pixels -- so the tint there
moves only slightly (B-R +0.2500 -> +0.2770 on the test) -- while the setting is aimed at the **surround** of a bright
source, which is where a small difference actually gets divided away. Whether that is enough on the reference scene is
a measurement, and it is the next thing to take.

163 checks pass. Two mechanisms have been added this session -- `source_glow` and `halation_source_colour` -- and both
are inert at their defaults, the second verified bit-identical.

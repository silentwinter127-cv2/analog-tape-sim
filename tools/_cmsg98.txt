**The fault, found by reading the line that caused it.** `CameraStage._halation` finished with a **fixed** tint:

    tint = np.array([1.05, 0.88, 1.05])
    return rgb + (a * 0.52) * halo[..., None] * tint[None, None, :]

The halo is built from a **luminance** mask, so it is colourless by construction, and the fixed tint then gives every
pixel it touches neutral-to-magenta light. **That is what divides a small chroma difference away**: a bright thin line
whose own colour is a small blue keeps its brightness and its contrast and loses its hue -- measured on the reference
scene, the pole came back at **B-R +0.000** against its own **+0.024**, and no value of any existing parameter
recovered it, because raising saturation cannot restore a difference that neutral light has divided out.

**So `halation` now takes its tint from the blown region itself** -- `rgb * hot`, averaged per channel and normalised
so its **luminance is unchanged** -- which means a scene with no blown source, or a neutral one, behaves exactly as
before and only a **coloured** source moves. The physical reason is the one `source_glow` already uses: light scattered
from a bright source keeps that source's spectrum.

**And the change is opt-in, which is verified rather than asserted.** A new parameter `halation_source_colour`
(default **0.0**) blends between the fixed lens tint and the source's own. At 0.0 the output is **bit-identical** to the
old body, checked with `np.array_equal` on four sources -- blue-white, white, warm-bright and sodium all return
`True`. At 1.0 the added colour follows the source in both directions:

    the blown source       its own B-R   halation 0.0   halation 1.0   follows
    blue-white             +0.2500       +0.00000       +0.00359       yes
    white                   0.0000       +0.00000       +0.00000       yes (stays neutral)
    warm-bright            -0.4500       +0.00000       -0.00676       yes
    sodium                 -0.7000       +0.00000       -0.00940       yes

**One test of mine was wrong and is recorded.** The first attempt used a warm patch of (1.00, 0.62, 0.25) and measured
no effect. The mask is `hot = clip((lum - 0.72) / 0.28, 0, None)`, so that patch's luma of **0.6914** is **below the
threshold** and the mechanism correctly did nothing. **A mechanism that never fires cannot be judged.**

**What is not established, and is stated rather than implied.** The end-to-end reading on the reference scene came back
with the pole's contrast at **0.0000** and a blown fraction of **0.20** -- i.e. the frame almost entirely white --
which is **inconsistent with the unit tests**, where the change is bit-identical at its default and moves only a small
amount at 1.0. So **that script is wrong, not the mechanism**, and until it is corrected the effect of the new
parameter on a real photograph is **unmeasured**. Reporting a number from a harness that is producing a 20% blown
fraction would be worse than reporting none.

**Two mechanisms have been added this session**: `source_glow`, and now `halation_source_colour`. Both are inert at
their defaults and the second is verified bit-identical. 163 checks pass, selftest clean, and the reproduction file
carries the settings from before this change until the end-to-end measurement is sound.

calibrate the row time-base against a worn VHS capture, and fix it being too ragged

The supplied material is a JVC SR-S388E capture of a worn karaoke cassette, with the
TBC toggled on and off while it was being recorded. That last detail is what makes it
useful: the same tape, the same deck, so any difference between stretches is the
deck's behaviour rather than the material's.

**Measuring this needed the right quantity, twice.** A first scan looked at how much a
frame's row-mean profile changed between samples and it flagged the karaoke sections --
but those move because scrolling lyrics change the content. Content motion is not
time-base error. The fix is to measure the displacement of edges rather than the level
of rows.

That attempt then tracked the strongest gradient in each row, and on real material the
strongest gradient in one row is a different object from the one in the next, so the
"track" jumped across the frame: reported spread 235 px on a frame 715 px wide, which
is the tracker describing scene content. Correlating each row against the local
vertical average fixed it, since the question is only how far a row has moved relative
to what surrounds it.

Measured that way, per-row displacement error grows along the tape, as a worn cassette
should:

    t=1000s   std 0.193 px (capture)   adjacent-row step 0.068   range  1.89
    t=1380s   std 0.462 px             adjacent-row step 0.136   range  2.97
    t=2000s   std 1.031 px             adjacent-row step 0.111   range 13.19

The capture is 2x upscaled from 720-wide native, so the signal-domain figures are half
those. Matching them against the model gives amounts of roughly 0.02 / 0.04 / 0.08 --
and the preset was using 0.9, about eleven times the worst case the material supports.
It now uses 0.12, and the measured values are recorded in the parameter's help text so
the next person does not have to re-derive them.

**A real defect the calibration exposed.** The ratio of the median adjacent-row step to
the overall spread is 0.108 in the real material: the displacement varies slowly enough
that a vertical edge *bends* rather than tears. The model's field gave 0.311 -- three
times too ragged. The smoothing kernel width is not something that can be reasoned to;
it was swept against the measured ratio, and 26 passes of a 5-tap kernel give 0.097.
Without this comparison the mechanism would have looked correct while producing
visibly wrong geometry, since the amplitude alone matched.

45 checks pass, 14 presets render.

Not done: the TBC on/off comparison itself. The user toggled it while recording, so the
material contains a genuine controlled experiment -- `player.tbc_enabled` and
`tbc_strength` claim to model that behaviour, and measuring the real difference between
the two stretches is the way to check the claim rather than assume it.

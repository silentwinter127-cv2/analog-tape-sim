add an MPEG-1 / Video CD coding branch

A fingerprint comparison of a supplied VCD frame against every preset put the closest
at a residual of 0.075 and the rest three to thirty times further away, with hue spread
the largest single gap. The honest reading was not "the presets are wrong" but "this is
a format the tool does not model": VCD is MPEG-1, and there was no MPEG-1 branch.

What the branch models, in the order the differences matter to the picture:

  * the quantisation matrices MPEG-1 specifies. Their shape is steep -- the highest
    frequency weighted about ten times the lowest -- which is where VCD's softness comes
    from: high frequencies are discarded *in a pattern*, not attenuated smoothly;
  * 4:2:0 chroma, both axes halved, against DV's 4:1:1 at 525/60;
  * temporal prediction with motion compensation, which is the mechanism behind the
    blocky ghosting people associate with the format. Error is not independent per
    frame: where the predictor fails (occlusion, a hard cut, a moving edge) the residual
    is large and the error is visible, and where it succeeds the frame is clean. The
    predictor uses the previous *decoded* luma, as a real decoder must -- predicting
    from the original would hide the accumulation;
  * MPEG-1's zigzag ordering, applied and inverted, because the matrices are indexed by
    scan position and skipping the scan would make their frequency weighting arbitrary.

Limitations stated rather than implied: no entropy coding, no rate control, no B-frames.
The bitrate parameter informs the quantiser scale and is not enforced, so a busy frame
shows more error than a real encoder's rate control would allow.

Verified on the codec alone:

    motion compensation    prediction error 0.00943 -> 0.00320
    quality control        error 0.051 / 0.034 / 0.027 at quality 0.5 / 1.0 / 2.0
    not lossless           per-frame error 0.033, stable across P-frames
    high frequency         reference frame at 360x220: 0.05127 -> 0.02991

**The preset does not yet match real VCD, and the gap is not a tuning problem.** Through
the whole chain the `vcd_352x240` preset gives a high-frequency figure of 0.209 against
the reference's 0.051, a residual of 2.292 that ranks it last rather than first -- the
opposite of the point of adding it. Measured in isolation the codec *reduces* high
frequency (0.05127 -> 0.02991 on the same reference frame), so the 0.209 is being
produced by an interaction rather than by the codec's own quantisation, and four
candidate causes were eliminated by measurement (chroma vertical averaging, colour
noise, camera noise, and the codec settings themselves -- dropping each changed the
figure by under 1%). What is left is that the codec is being fed something different
from what the isolated test feeds it, and that needs finding before the preset can be
claimed to work.

47 checks pass, 15 presets render. `vcd_width` and `vcd_height` are declared with a
physical range pinned to the standard values, because they describe what MPEG-1 codes
rather than being a control.
